📚 Vocabulary & Memory Verse
💡 ESL Tip: Covenant is a key Bible word. It means a serious, binding agreement between God and people — deeper than a normal contract. God made covenants with Noah, Abraham, and here with Israel at Sinai. The Ten Commandments are given INSIDE God's covenant — they describe how covenant people should live.
1
Write the memory verse from Matthew 22:37,39 in full. Then explain in your own words what Jesus means by "love your neighbour as yourself." 3 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
"Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind… Love your neighbour as yourself." (Matthew 22:37,39 NIV). "Love your neighbour as yourself" means: treat other people the same way you would want to be treated. The "neighbour" is not just the person next door — Jesus made clear (in the Parable of the Good Samaritan) that "neighbour" means any person you are able to help, even someone from a completely different background. Loving yourself here is the standard, not a command — Jesus assumes you naturally care for your own wellbeing and says: apply that same care to others.
2
Match each vocabulary word to its correct definition: commandment · covenant · idolatry · sabbath · covet 5 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
commandment = a strong law or rule given by God — not just advice but a definite instruction. covenant = a serious, binding agreement between God and people — deeper than a contract, more like a treaty. idolatry = worshipping an idol (a statue or image) instead of the real God, or treating something as more important than God. sabbath = the seventh day set aside by God as a day of rest — built into creation from Genesis 2. covet = to want what belongs to someone else in a jealous, unhealthy way — even before any action is taken.

🔍 Understanding & Explanation
4
The lesson says God gave Israel the commandments AFTER rescuing them from Egypt — not before. Explain why the ORDER matters. What does this tell us about God's character and his relationship with Israel? 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
The order matters enormously. Before giving any commandment, God says: "I am the LORD your God, who brought you OUT OF EGYPT, out of the land of slavery" (Exodus 20:2). Rescue came FIRST. This means the commandments are not a way to earn God's love — Israel was already loved and already saved. The law was given as a guide for HOW TO LIVE as God's rescued people, not as a test they had to pass to receive his grace. This tells us God's character is primarily gracious and saving — law comes as a gift to people he already loves, not as a barrier to overcome.
5
The commandments are divided into two groups: 1-4 (about God) and 5-10 (about people). Choose ONE commandment from each group and explain: (a) what it specifically protects or preserves, and (b) what would happen to a community if everyone broke that commandment. 6 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Examples: Commandment 4 (Sabbath): (a) It protects rest and human dignity — prevents people from being worked like machines without break. It also reminds people that God provides and that work is not the source of life. (b) If everyone ignored it, workers would be exploited without limits, poverty and exhaustion would increase, and people would become defined only by their productivity. Commandment 8 (Do not steal): (a) It protects property and the trust between people — the right of every person to what belongs to them. (b) If everyone stole freely, no one would be able to build wealth, plan for the future, or trust their neighbours. Trade and community would break down completely. Accept any pair with clear reasoning.
6
Commandment 10 (do not covet) is described as "the only commandment about a thought, not an action." Why is it significant that God commands something about what we DESIRE, not just what we DO? 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Most human laws can only deal with actions — a government can punish someone for stealing but not for wanting to steal. Commandment 10 shows that God is concerned not just with behaviour but with the desires of the heart. This is significant because: (1) Most wrongdoing starts with coveting — wanting what is not ours leads to stealing, lying, and even murder. (2) It shows that character, not just conduct, matters to God. (3) The New Testament (Matthew 5) develops this further — Jesus says that anger is the root of murder, lust is the root of adultery. The commandment points to the importance of inner transformation, not just outer conformity. The antidote to coveting is contentment — being grateful for what you have.

7
The lesson says "the heart behind God's law is always love." Using THREE specific commandments as evidence, explain how each one is an expression of love — either love for God or love for people. Write at least 5 sentences. 6 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
The Ten Commandments are not restrictions for their own sake — they are love expressed as protection. Commandment 1 (no other gods): This expresses love for God by refusing to give his place to anything lesser — money, power, pleasure. Anything that replaces God eventually disappoints and enslaves people; the commandment protects Israel from this bondage. Expository Writing —

Day 13 Difficulty LevelsKS3 Level 6
🟢 EasySection A — Vocabulary and structure identification. Use your lesson notes.
📚 Vocabulary & Structure
💡 ESL Tip: Expository comes from Latin "exponere" — to set out or explain. Expository writing is NOT creative writing. It is writing that INFORMS and EXPLAINS. A science report, a history essay, a how-to guide — all are expository. Your job is to be clear, organised, and factual.
1
Define each term in your own words and give one example of how you would use it in an essay: thesis statement · topic sentence · cohesive device · formal register 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
thesis statement: 1-2 sentences at the end of the introduction that state the main point of the essay. Example: "This essay will explain how the Ten Commandments shaped both personal behaviour and community life in ancient Israel." topic sentence: The first sentence of a body paragraph — states what that paragraph is about. Example: "The first four commandments established Israel's exclusive relationship with God." cohesive device: A word or phrase that connects sentences or paragraphs. Example: "Furthermore," "However," "As a result," "In addition." formal register: Professional academic writing style — no slang, no contractions. Example: informal "don't" → formal "do not"; informal "loads of" → formal "a significant number of."
2
Read this paragraph and label: (a) the topic sentence, (b) two supporting details, (c) a cohesive device, and (d) a linking sentence. 5 marks

"Commandments five through ten provided ancient Israel with a framework for social order. The prohibition against murder (Commandment 6) protected human life, which the Bible considers precious because all people are made in God's image. Furthermore, the command against false testimony (Commandment 9) protected individuals' reputations and ensured that courts could function fairly. As a result, communities that followed these laws would have experienced far less conflict and injustice than those without them. This social dimension of the commandments connects directly to Jesus' teaching that 'love your neighbour as yourself.'"
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
(a) Topic sentence: "Commandments five through ten provided ancient Israel with a framework for social order." — This is the opening sentence that tells the reader what the paragraph is about. (b) Supporting detail 1: "The prohibition against murder... all people are made in God's image." Supporting detail 2: "the command against false testimony... courts could function fairly." (c) Cohesive device: "Furthermore" (adds another supporting point) and "As a result" (shows consequence). (d) Linking sentence: The last sentence — "This social dimension... 'love your neighbour as yourself'" — connects this paragraph's content to the broader theme of the essay.
3
List the three parts of an expository essay and state what goes in EACH part. 3 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Introduction: Hook (interesting first sentence) + background (2-3 sentences of context) + thesis statement (main point of the essay, 1-2 sentences). Body paragraphs: Each paragraph has one idea — topic sentence + 2-3 supporting details (facts, evidence, examples) + a linking sentence to the next paragraph. A KS3 essay typically has 2-4 body paragraphs. Conclusion: Restate the thesis in new words + briefly summarise the main points + a final thought about why this matters. NEVER introduce new ideas in the conclusion.

6
A student says: "3x + 4y = 7xy." Explain clearly why this is WRONG. What is the correct answer and why? 3 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
The student is wrong because 3x and 4y are NOT like terms — they have different variables (x and y). You can only add or subtract terms with the same variable. 3x + 4y cannot be simplified further — the answer stays as 3x + 4y. Writing "7xy" is also wrong because xy means x multiplied by y — a completely different operation. The rule is: to add or subtract algebraic terms, they must have exactly the same variable (and same power). 3 apples + 4 oranges ≠ 7 "apple-oranges."
7
An expression is 3n + 5. Its value is 20 when a certain value of n is used. What is that value of n? Show your working. Then check your answer by substituting back in. 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
If 3n + 5 = 20, then 3n = 20 − 5 = 15. So n = 15 ÷ 3 = 5. Check: 3(5) + 5 = 15 + 5 = 20 ✓. (Note: this is an early introduction to solving equations — finding the value that makes an expression equal a particular number.)

Mixtures —
Foundations
Pure vs mixed · Filtration · Evaporation · Choosing the right technique
Day 13 Difficulty LevelsKS3 Level 6
🟢 EasySection A — Vocabulary and identification. Use your lesson notes if needed.
📚 Vocabulary & Pure vs Mixed
💡 ESL Tip: Soluble = able to dissolve. Insoluble = NOT able to dissolve. This is the key word in choosing a separation technique. Sand is INSOLUBLE — use filtration. Salt is SOLUBLE — you cannot filter it out. Use evaporation or distillation instead.
1
Define each term and give one example: mixture · pure substance · soluble · insoluble · solute · solvent · solution 7 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
mixture: two or more substances mixed together but not chemically joined. Example: salt water. pure substance: only one type of substance, with fixed properties. Example: pure water. soluble: able to dissolve in a liquid. Example: sugar is soluble in water. insoluble: does NOT dissolve in a liquid. Example: sand is insoluble in water. solute: the substance that dissolves. Example: salt (in salt water). solvent: the liquid in which the solute dissolves. Example: water (in salt water). solution: the mixture formed when a solute dissolves in a solvent. Example: salt water.
2
Sort these substances into two columns — MIXTURE or PURE SUBSTANCE: air · pure gold · sea water · pure salt (NaCl) · ink · pure water · muddy water · pure iron · sand + gravel 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
MIXTURE: air (nitrogen + oxygen + other gases), sea water (water + dissolved salts + minerals), ink (mixture of dyes and water), muddy water (water + solid particles), sand + gravel (two different solids mixed). PURE SUBSTANCE: pure gold (only gold atoms), pure salt/NaCl (only sodium chloride), pure water (only H₂O), pure iron (only iron atoms).
3
Describe the process of filtration step by step. Name the equipment used and explain what happens to the solid and the liquid. 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Equipment: filter funnel, filter paper, conical flask (or beaker), stand and clamp (optional). Process: Fold filter paper into a cone and place inside the funnel. Put the funnel over the collecting flask. Slowly pour the mixture (e.g. muddy water) into the funnel. What happens to the solid (residue): The solid particles (e.g. mud/sand) are too large to pass through the tiny holes in the filter paper — they collect in the funnel and stay in the paper. What happens to the liquid (filtrate): The liquid (clear water) is small enough to pass through the holes in the filter paper — it drips through into the collecting flask below.
Section B
🟡 MediumSection B — Comparing techniques and explaining choices.
🔍 Comparing Techniques
4
Explain the difference between evaporation and distillation. For each technique: describe the process, state what you collect at the end, and give one specific use. 6 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
EVAPORATION — Process: heat the solution in an evaporating dish; the liquid turns to steam and escapes into the air. What you collect: the solid — e.g. salt crystals remain in the dish after the water has evaporated. Use: recovering salt from sea water / salt water. DISTILLATION — Process: heat the solution in a flask; the liquid boils and turns to vapour. The vapour travels through a condenser (cool tube) and is cooled back into a pure liquid, which is collected in a separate beaker. What you collect: the liquid — pure water. The salt or solid stays behind in the flask. Use: producing pure (distilled) water from salt water; also used to separate two liquids with different boiling points (e.g. alcohol from water). Key difference: evaporation lets the liquid escape; distillation collects the liquid.
5
For each mixture below, state which separation technique you would use AND explain why it is the most appropriate choice: 6 marks

a) Chalk powder mixed with water
b) Salt dissolved in water (you want pure water)
c) Salt dissolved in water (you want the salt)
d) A mixture of two different food colourings in water
e) Soil mixed with water
f) Alcohol mixed with water (alcohol boils at 78°C, water at 100°C)
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
a) Filtration — chalk is insoluble in water; the chalk particles are too large to pass through filter paper. b) Distillation — to collect pure water, you need to boil it, collect the steam, and condense it back to liquid in a separate container. c) Evaporation — heat the solution; the water evaporates and escapes; the salt remains as dry crystals in the dish. d) Chromatography — different food colouring dyes will travel at different speeds up the chromatography paper, separating them into distinct bands. e) Filtration — soil particles are insoluble; filter paper will catch them while clean water passes through. f) Distillation — alcohol boils at 78°C and water at 100°C; heat to 78°C and the alcohol vapour rises first and can be collected separately, leaving water behind.

🏆 Scientific Reasoning
6
A scientist has a mixture containing THREE components: sand (insoluble solid), salt (soluble solid), and water (liquid). She wants to separate all three components as cleanly as possible.

(a) Describe the complete procedure she should follow, step by step.
(b) State which technique is used at each step and why.
(c) At the end, what does she have in each container?
(d) Why is filtration done BEFORE evaporation in this procedure? What would happen if you reversed the order? 9 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
(a) Step 1: Set up a filter funnel with filter paper over a conical flask. Pour the mixture (sand + salt + water) through the filter paper. Step 2: Collect the salt water (filtrate) that passes through in the flask. The sand remains in the filter paper. Step 3: Pour the salt water into an evaporating dish. Heat gently until all the water evaporates. Step 4: The salt crystals remain in the evaporating dish. Remove the dish from heat and allow to cool. (b) Step 1-2: Filtration — because sand is insoluble; particle size too large for filter paper. Step 3-4: Evaporation — because salt is dissolved in the water; heating removes the water, leaving dry salt. (c) Container 1 (filter paper): dry sand. Container 2 (evaporating dish): white salt crystals. Surroundings: water vapour has escaped into the air. (d) Filtration must come first because the sand needs to be removed while the liquid is still present to carry it through the filter. If you evaporated first, the sand and salt would both remain in the evaporating dish as a mixed solid — and then you would have no way to separate sand from salt, as they are both insoluble in water without the water present. The liquid is essential for filtration to work.

Population & Settlement —
Foundations
Why people settle · Settlement factors · Rural vs Urban · Key vocabulary
Day 13 Difficulty LevelsKS3 Level 6
🟢 EasySection A — Vocabulary and key facts. Use your lesson notes if needed.
📚 Vocabulary & Settlement Factors
💡 ESL Tip: Population density = how many people live in a given area. We measure it as "people per km²." A city like Tokyo has thousands of people per km² (HIGH density). The Sahara Desert has less than 1 person per km² (LOW density). The REASON for this difference is always linked to the physical environment — water, climate, fertile land.
1
Define each term and give one example: settlement · population density · rural · urban · urbanisation · migration 6 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
settlement: a place where people live permanently — from a tiny hamlet to a huge city. Example: ancient Rome. population density: how many people live in a given area (people per km²). High density = crowded; low density = sparse. Example: Tokyo has one of the highest population densities in the world. rural: relating to the countryside — farmland, small villages, forests. Main work: farming and land-based activities. Example: a small farming village in Pakistan. urban: relating to cities and large towns. High density, many services. Example: Karachi, London, New York. urbanisation: the process of more and more people moving from rural areas to cities. Example: in 1800 only ~3% lived in cities; today over 55% do. migration: movement of people from one place to another to live — internal (within one country) or international (between countries). Example: people moving from rural Pakistan to Karachi.
2
The lesson gives six main reasons why people settle in a particular place. List all six and give a brief explanation of each. 6 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
1. Water supply — Every settlement needs fresh water for drinking, cooking, and farming. Rivers and springs were the main reason ancient peoples stopped moving. 2. Fertile land — Flat, nutrient-rich soil allows farming to feed large numbers of people. River valleys like the Nile had especially rich soil from annual flood deposits. 3. Natural defence — Hills, cliffs, rivers, and coastlines could protect communities from attack. Medieval towns were built on high ground or surrounded by water for this reason. 4. Mild climate — Moderate temperatures and reliable rainfall make farming and daily life easier. Extreme environments (deserts, polar regions) have very low population density because they are hard to survive in. 5. Transport routes — Settlements grew at river crossings, ports, and road junctions where trade and travel were easy. Trade brought wealth. 6. Natural resources — Places with useful materials (coal, forests, fish, minerals) attracted workers and grew into towns and cities.

Connect to Week 6 and Week 7: Use what you know about physical geography (rivers, deserts, climate) to explain WHY some regions of the world have very HIGH population density and others have very LOW population density. Give a specific example of each. 5 marks
🔗 Cross-week connection: Week 6 — rivers, deserts, fertile land. Week 7 — climate zones, biomes, temperature and rainfall.
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
High density regions: The Ganges Plain in India and Bangladesh has one of the world's highest population densities. Physical reasons: the Ganges River deposits fertile silt, providing excellent farmland; the monsoon climate brings regular rainfall; the flat terrain allows large-scale farming and easy movement and trade. All six settlement factors — water, fertile land, mild climate, transport, resources — are present. Low density regions: The Sahara Desert in North Africa has extremely low population density (less than 1 person per km² in most areas). Physical reasons: almost no rainfall means farming is impossible; extreme heat makes outdoor work dangerous; no rivers mean no water supply; no fertile soil. These same factors explain why the Amazon Rainforest interior, the Arctic, and Himalayan mountain peaks are also sparsely populated — the physical environment makes permanent settlement and farming extremely difficult. The pattern is clear: people settle where physical geography gives them the best chance of survival and food production.

"The same factors that caused ancient civilisations to settle near rivers are still shaping where people live today." Evaluate this statement using evidence from BOTH this week's geography lesson AND at least TWO other weeks studied this term (Weeks 1-7). Write at least 7 sentences. 7 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
The statement is strongly supported by both historical and geographical evidence across multiple weeks of study. The fundamental factors that drew ancient people to settle — water, fertile land, mild climate, defence, and trade routes — remain the core determinants of population density on Earth today. Week 2 connection (Egypt and Mesopotamia): The Nile's annual predictable flood made Egypt the most stable agricultural state in the ancient world, allowing a population of millions to be fed consistently. Today, Egypt's population is still overwhelmingly concentrated in the Nile Valley and Delta — less than 5% of Egypt's land area is inhabited, almost entirely along the same river. This is not coincidence: the same water and soil that drew settlement 5,000 years ago still dictates where 100 million people can live. Week 6 connection (World regions): The Amazon Rainforest has extraordinarily high biodiversity but low human population density — because despite abundant rainfall, the dense jungle, disease, and difficulty of farming on nutrient-poor rainforest soil made large-scale settlement historically challenging. By contrast, the river plains of South and Southeast Asia (the Indus and Mekong deltas) continue to support billions of people, as they have for thousands of years. Counterargument: Technology has somewhat weakened the connection — irrigation allows desert cities like Dubai or Las Vegas to exist, and transport infrastructure means modern cities can import food and water from elsewhere. However, even these cities depend ultimately on resources transported from places that have natural fertility. The fundamental factors have not changed; technology has made some of them more flexible but not obsolete. The statement is therefore largely accurate: physical geography set the original patterns, and human settlement today still largely follows them.
