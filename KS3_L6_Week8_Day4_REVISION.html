🌐 Big Ideas That Connect Everything This Week
🔗 Order from complexity
The commandments bring moral order to human relationships. Algebra brings mathematical order to unknown quantities. Separation techniques bring physical order to mixed substances. Expository writing brings structural order to ideas. Every subject this week is about finding clarity within complexity.
🔗 Unknown things can be found
Maths: a variable represents an unknown number that substitution reveals. Science: chromatography reveals the unknown components of a mixture. Geography: push/pull factors reveal the hidden reasons behind a person's decision to migrate. Finding what is hidden is a theme across the week.
🔗 What came first matters
Bible: grace came before law — God rescued Israel before giving commandments. Geography: water supply came before settlement — civilisations grew where water already existed. English: background comes before thesis — context must be set before the argument. Order and sequence shape meaning.
🔗 The whole depends on its parts working together
Bible: all ten commandments together form one vision of love. Maths: like terms must be grouped correctly for an expression to simplify properly. English: every paragraph must connect to the thesis. Geography: settlement factors (water, land, climate, defence) combine, rather than acting alone, to determine where people live.

🏆 Final Extended Question
★★
Choose ONE of these essay questions and write a full response (minimum 8 sentences): 15 marks

Option A: "The most surprising thing about the Ten Commandments is what comes BEFORE Commandment 1." Discuss what Exodus 20:2 reveals and why its placement matters.
Option B: "Commandment 10 (do not covet) is the most important commandment because it addresses the root of all the others." Do you agree?

💡 Show Marking Guidance ▼

Option C — Jesus raising the bar: Award marks for: "you have heard it said... but I tell you" (Matthew 5) shows Jesus moving from outward act to inward state — a HIGHER standard, not a lower one. Avoiding murder is easier than avoiding ongoing anger; avoiding the act of adultery is easier than avoiding lust. Matthew 22's summary (love God, love neighbour) is also a higher and harder standard than rule-following, because it cannot be faked or technically satisfied — it requires actual transformation of the heart.

📝 Structure & Vocabulary Test
★
Define each of these 10 terms precisely AND use each correctly in one sentence of your own. 15 marks

1. expository   2. thesis statement   3. topic sentence   4. supporting detail   5. cohesive device   6. conclusion   7. formal register   8. audience   9. hook   10. linking sentence
💡 Show Answers ▼
1. expository — writing that explains/informs clearly, without personal opinion. 2. thesis statement — 1-2 sentences stating the essay's main point, at the end of the introduction. 3. topic sentence — the first sentence of a body paragraph, stating what that paragraph is about. 4. supporting detail — facts/evidence/examples that prove the topic sentence. 5. cohesive device — words connecting sentences/paragraphs (e.g. furthermore, however). 6. conclusion — final paragraph; summarises and closes; no new ideas. 7. formal register — professional academic style; no slang or contractions. 8. audience — the person/people who will read the writing. 9. hook — an engaging opening sentence that draws the reader in. 10. linking sentence — the final sentence of a body paragraph, connecting to the next paragraph's idea.

🏆 Final Challenge Problem
★★
A school is designing a rectangular playground. The length is (4x+3) metres and the width is (2x−1) metres. (a) Write a simplified expression for the perimeter. (b) Write an expanded expression for the area. (c) The school decides x = 7. Find the actual length, width, perimeter, and area. (d) Safety fencing costs £12 per metre for the perimeter. What is the total fencing cost? 8 marks
💡 Show Answer ▼
(a) Perimeter = 2(4x+3)+2(2x−1) = 8x+6+4x−2 = 12x+4 metres. (b) Area = (4x+3)(2x−1) = 8x²−4x+6x−3 = 8x²+2x−3 m². (c) When x=7: Length = 4(7)+3 = 31m. Width = 2(7)−1 = 13m. Perimeter = 12(7)+4 = 84+4 = 88m. Area = 8(49)+2(7)−3 = 392+14−3 = 403 m² (or check: 31×13=403 ✓). (d) Fencing cost = 88 × £12 = £1,056.

🎯 Multiple Choice Quiz
🧪 Technique Matching Challenge
★
From memory — match each mixture to the BEST separation technique(s) and explain your reasoning in one sentence each: 10 marks

a) Gravel mixed with water
b) Two food dyes mixed in a solution
c) Sugar dissolved in water (want the sugar)
d) A mixture of water and ethanol (different boiling points)
e) Sand, salt, and water all mixed together
💡 Show Guidance ▼
a) Filtration — gravel is insoluble and too large to pass through filter paper. b) Chromatography — the dyes will travel at different speeds, separating into distinct bands. c) Evaporation — heating removes the water, leaving dry sugar crystals behind. d) Distillation — the liquid with the lower boiling point (ethanol, ~78°C) vaporises first and can be collected separately from water (100°C). e) Filtration then evaporation — first filter out the insoluble sand; then evaporate the remaining salt water to collect the salt.
🌍 Cross-Subject Final Challenge
★
Connect this week's science to this week's geography: explain in a short paragraph (5-6 sentences) how the science of separation techniques (specifically distillation) has changed where humans can settle, using specific evidence from both subjects. 8 marks
💡 Show Guidance ▼
For most of human history, settlement required access to a natural fresh water source, which is why every ancient civilisation grew up beside a river. Today, the science of distillation — heating salt water, collecting the vapour, and condensing it back into pure liquid — allows fresh water to be created from the ocean itself. Countries like Saudi Arabia and the UAE, located in extremely arid desert regions with virtually no rainfall, now host some of the fastest-growing cities in the world precisely because desalination technology provides the water that geography alone could not. This represents a genuine shift in the relationship between physical geography and human settlement: water no longer needs to occur naturally where people wish to live, as long as energy and technology are available to produce it. However, this technological solution remains expensive and energy-intensive, meaning that for the majority of the world's population, the ancient rule still holds — settlement still follows water.

🏆 Final Extended Geography Question
★★
Choose ONE question and write a full response (minimum 8 sentences): 12 marks

Option A: "Push and pull factors always work together — no migration decision is made for only one reason." Explain and evaluate this claim using a specific case study.

Option B: "Urbanisation is the single most important demographic change of the last 200 years." Argue for or against, using specific data from this week's lesson.

Option C: Using the six settlement factors from this week, explain why a specific real city (choose one you know) developed where it did.
💡 Show Marking Guidance ▼
Option A: Award for: explanation that push and pull factors typically combine — e.g. a family facing drought (push) AND knowing relatives have better jobs elsewhere (pull) is far more likely to migrate than a family facing only one factor. Most real migration decisions involve a combination, and the relative WEIGHT of push vs pull varies by situation (refugees = push-dominant; economic migrants = often more pull-dominant, though push factors like poverty are usually present too). Option B: Award for: data — 3% urban (1800) to 55%+ today represents possibly the largest demographic shift in human history within 200 years; counter-evidence — population growth itself (1bn to 8bn) might be considered equally or more significant since it is the precondition for urbanisation; medical/health advances also enabled both trends. A nuanced answer recognises urbanisation as deeply important but considers it alongside other major demographic shifts. Option C: Award for: choice of a real city (e.g. Karachi — port/transport route + water access from Arabian Sea; London — Thames river crossing + trade route + relatively mild climate; Cairo — Nile water + fertile land + defensible desert surroundings) with at least 3 of the six factors explicitly applied with specific reasoning.
