Day 2Builds on Day 1KS3 Level 6
🟢 Easy Section A — Key terms check. Can you remember and use yesterday's vocabulary?
🔄 Vocabulary Retrieval
💡 ESL Tip: "Characterisation" is how a writer shows us what a character is like. Good writers do NOT say "she was brave" — they SHOW it through actions, dialogue, or descriptions.
1
What is "characterisation"? Name TWO ways a writer can reveal a character's personality to the reader. 3 marks
2
What is the difference between what is STATED in a text and what is IMPLIED? Give one example of each. 2 marks
3
Name THREE types of mood that an author might create in a story. 1 mark
4
Write a complete PEEL paragraph about a character you know from a book, film, or story. Use all four stages of PEEL. 4 marks
P = point about the character, E = a quote or example, E = explain what it tells us, L = link to the theme or author's intent.
Section B
🟡 Medium Section B — New extract. Apply your inference and analysis skills.
📄 New Reading Extract
📝
Read this new extract carefully before answering Questions 5–9:

"The old man sat at the end of the jetty, watching the boats return. He counted them, one by one, the way he always did. There were seven. There should have been eight. He said nothing. He simply folded his hands in his lap and looked out at the flat, indifferent sea."
5
What can you INFER about what has happened to the eighth boat? Use evidence from the text. 3 marks
6
The author describes the sea as "flat" and "indifferent." What does "indifferent" mean in this context? What effect does this word choice have on the reader? 3 marks
ESL Tip: "Indifferent" means not caring, having no feelings either way. Think about why describing the sea this way makes the scene feel more or less emotional.
7
The man "said nothing" and "simply folded his hands." What does the author imply about the man's emotional state through these ACTIONS rather than direct description? 3 marks
8
What do you think the THEME of this story might be? Write your theme as a statement (not just one word) and explain your reasoning with evidence from the text. 3 marks
A theme statement sounds like: "The story explores how grief can make a person feel powerless..." rather than just writing "sadness."
9
Write a PEEL paragraph analysing how the author uses setting to reflect the old man's emotions. 4 marks
Section C
🔴 Hard Section C — High-level analysis. Think about craft, technique, and authorial choices.
🖊️ Extended Analysis
10
Compare the TWO extracts from Day 1 (Maya at the cliff) and Day 2 (the old man at the jetty). Both characters are waiting. How does each author create a different mood despite a similar situation? Identify at least TWO specific language choices from each extract. Write at least 6 sentences. 6 marks
11
Explain the difference between a writer's THEME and a writer's MESSAGE. Are they always the same thing? Use ONE of the extracts to illustrate your answer. 4 marks
ESL Tip: A theme is a big topic (e.g. "loss"). A message is what the writer seems to be saying ABOUT that topic (e.g. "loss is something that cannot be spoken about, only felt").
12
The old man extract uses only 5 sentences to create a powerful emotional effect. Write your own 5-sentence extract using the same techniques: (a) a character waiting or watching, (b) a detail that implies something has gone wrong, (c) silence or stillness as characterisation, (d) a setting word that reflects emotion. Then annotate your writing — label where you used each technique. 8 marks
