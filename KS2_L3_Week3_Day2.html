📖 Part B — Read & Analyse (10 marks)
"Then God said to Noah, 'I have decided to put an end to all people, for the earth is filled with violence because of them… Make yourself an ark of cypress wood…' Noah did everything just as God commanded him." — Genesis 6:13–14, 22
a) God tells Noah about the flood BEFORE it happens. Why do you think God warned Noah instead of sending the flood without warning? What does this tell us about God's character?
God warned Noah because He is fair and caring. He did not want to destroy a righteous person without warning. This shows that God looks at the heart of individuals — He rewards those who obey Him. It tells us God is not randomly cruel; He acts with justice and love.
b) "Noah did everything just as God commanded him." This is said about Noah BEFORE the flood came. Noah had never seen a flood — he had to trust completely. What does this tell us about Noah's faith?
Noah's faith was very strong. He obeyed God even though he could not see the flood coming — he had never experienced anything like it before. People laughed at him, making it even harder. True faith means trusting God even when things seem impossible or others disagree.
c) People around Noah laughed at him while he built the ark. How do you think Noah felt? How did he keep going? Use what you know about trust and obedience.
Noah may have felt lonely, embarrassed or doubted himself at times. But he kept going because his trust in God was stronger than his fear of what others thought. Obedience to God meant he focused on the task, not the laughter. This shows real courage — doing what is right even when it is hard.
d) God made a covenant (promise) after the flood. Why is it important that God used a visible sign — the rainbow — for this promise? What does the rainbow remind us every time we see it?
God used the rainbow as a visible sign so people could SEE the promise, not just hear it. A visible sign makes the promise feel real and permanent. Every time we see a rainbow, it is a reminder that God keeps His covenants — He does not break His promises. It shows that God's love reaches all the earth.

📖 Read the Passage Again
Zara and the Old Forest

Zara was eight years old. She lived in a small village near a very old forest. One morning, she woke up early and walked into the forest alone. The trees were tall and dark. Strange sounds came from the leaves above.

Zara felt nervous, but she kept walking. She wanted to find the old stone bridge her grandmother had told her about. "It grants one wish," her grandmother had said.

After a long walk, Zara heard the sound of water. She followed it and — there it was! A beautiful stone bridge covered in green moss. She closed her eyes and made her wish. She wished her sick father would get better.

When she opened her eyes, a small blue bird landed on her hand and sang. Zara smiled. Somehow, she felt sure everything would be alright.

🔍 Part A — Deep Analysis (10 marks)
a) "The trees were tall and dark." What does this description tell us about how Zara might be feeling? What effect does it create for the reader?
The description "tall and dark" creates a feeling of danger or mystery. It suggests the forest is threatening or unfamiliar. For the reader, it builds tension — we feel nervous alongside Zara. The writer uses this deliberately to show that Zara's journey is not easy; it takes real courage to keep walking.
b) "Zara felt nervous, but she kept walking." Why is the word "but" so important here? What does it tell us about Zara's character?
The word "but" shows contrast — it tells us that despite feeling scared, Zara pushed through. This single word reveals her character: she is brave and determined. She could have turned back, but her love for her father and her hope in the wish made her continue. The word "but" is doing a lot of work in just one sentence.
c) The writer uses a dash (—) in: "there it was!" What effect does this punctuation create? Why might the writer have chosen a dash rather than a comma?
The dash creates a sudden pause before the reveal — it builds excitement and suspense. The reader holds their breath for a moment. A comma would feel smoother and less dramatic. The dash mimics the feeling of Zara suddenly spotting the bridge, making the reader feel the surprise alongside her.
d) At the end, Zara "somehow felt sure everything would be alright." The writer does NOT tell us if the wish came true. Why might the writer have chosen to end the story this way?
The ambiguous ending is powerful because it focuses on Zara's FEELING rather than facts. Hope is more uplifting than certainty. The reader is left to believe the wish may come true, which is more emotionally satisfying. It also suggests that sometimes what matters most is trust and hope — not proof. This is a mature, thoughtful ending for a children's story.

🔬 Part A — Experiment Analysis (10 marks)
📋 Experiment Results — Four Plants
PlantConditionWhat happened after 5 days
ANo waterLeaves shrivelled and drooped. Stem became thin.
BNo lightGrew tall and thin. Leaves turned pale yellow.
CNo nutrients (soil)Survived but grew slowly. Leaves pale green.
DAll needs metGrew 12cm. Dark green leaves. Healthy.
a) Plant B grew TALLER without light — this seems strange! Why would a plant grow taller when it has NO sunlight? What was it trying to do?
Plant B grew taller because it was REACHING for light — this is called etiolation. The plant used its stored energy to grow upward, trying to find a light source. It got taller but weaker because without light, it could not make food through photosynthesis. The yellow colour shows it could not produce chlorophyll without sunlight.
b) Plant C survived without soil but grew pale and slowly. Which part of the plant was most affected, and why?
The ROOTS were most affected. Roots absorb nutrients (minerals) from the soil that the plant needs for healthy growth. Without these nutrients, the leaves turned pale green because the plant could not make enough chlorophyll. Chlorophyll is the green pigment that helps plants photosynthesise and grow strong.
c) Write a CONCLUSION sentence for this experiment. Use the words "photosynthesis" and "all four needs" in your sentence.
This experiment proves that plants need all four things — sunlight, water, nutrients and space — to grow healthily, because without any one of them, the plant's ability to carry out photosynthesis and grow properly is seriously affected.

🌍 Part C — Critical Thinking (10 marks)
a) A map shows a place from ABOVE. Why is this more useful than a side-view photograph for finding your way? Give TWO specific reasons.
Reason 1: A top-down view shows the exact layout of roads, parks and buildings relative to each other — you can see which direction to travel. Reason 2: A side-view photo is blocked by buildings in front — you cannot see what is behind them. A map shows ALL places at once without anything being hidden.
b) Maps use symbols instead of drawing everything in detail. Give THREE reasons why symbols are better than detailed drawings on a map.
1) Symbols save space — you can fit more information in a small area. 2) Symbols are quick to read — a trained eye spots a hospital cross instantly. 3) Symbols work for all languages — a tree icon means park whether you speak English, Urdu or French. Without symbols, maps would be cluttered and impossible to read quickly.
c) EXTENDED: You want to build a new playground on the map above. Using compass directions and all the places shown, recommend the BEST location. Explain your reasoning using directions.
Model answer: I recommend the area north of the Market, between the Park and the School. It is centrally located — the School is to the East and the Park to the West, making it easy for children from the school to reach going West, and for families using the Park to reach going East. The Hospital is to the South, which is useful in case of accidents. This central northern position means it is accessible from all four compass directions.
