📚 Week 7 — Moses & the Exodus
Week 7
1
Write the memory verse Exodus 14:14 from memory, and explain what it teaches about the Israelites' role versus God's role at the Red Sea. 5 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
"The LORD will fight for you; you need only to be still." (Exodus 14:14, NIV). This verse, spoken as the Israelites are trapped between the sea and Pharaoh's army, teaches that in this moment their role is NOT to fight or find their own escape, but to trust and remain calm while God acts on their behalf — a striking contrast to the courage and action expected elsewhere in Scripture (e.g. Joshua's story), showing that faith sometimes means active trust in waiting, not just action.
2
Explain the significance of Passover in the Exodus story. 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Passover marks the final plague against Egypt, where the Israelites were instructed to mark their doorframes with lamb's blood so that the plague of death would "pass over" their households, sparing their firstborn while it struck Egyptian households. This event directly led to Pharaoh releasing the Israelites from slavery, and it became a lasting yearly festival for the Israelites to remember God's deliverance.

Week 9 — Joshua
⚔️ Week 9 — Joshua: Courage & Promise
Week 9
4
Write the memory verse Joshua 1:9 from memory, and explain what it commands Joshua to do, and the reason given for this command. 5 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
"Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged, for the LORD your God will be with you wherever you go." (Joshua 1:9, NIV). Joshua is commanded to be strong and courageous, and specifically NOT to be afraid or discouraged, as he takes over leadership from Moses. The reason given is God's PRESENCE — "the LORD your God will be with you wherever you go" — courage here is grounded not in Joshua's own ability, but in the promise of God's ongoing presence.
Synthesis
🏆 Extended Synthesis Question
5
Compare the kind of faith shown at the Red Sea ("be still," Week 7) with the kind of courage commanded to Joshua ("be strong and courageous," Week 9). Using the Ten Commandments (Week 8) as the link between them, explain how God's law given through Moses shapes the kind of life Joshua is meant to lead into the Promised Land. Write at least 6 sentences. 8 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
At the Red Sea, the Israelites are commanded to "be still" and trust God to act on their behalf — a faith of stillness and dependence in a moment where no human action could save them. By contrast, Joshua is commanded to "be strong and courageous" — a faith of BOLD ACTION as he leads the people into the Promised Land. These are not contradictory but complementary: both instructions are grounded in the same reality of God's presence ("the LORD will fight for you" / "the LORD your God will be with you"), showing that biblical courage is not self-reliance but confidence rooted in God's nearness, whether that calls for stillness or action depending on the situation. The Ten Commandments, given between these two events, provide the moral framework within which this courage and trust are meant to operate — Joshua isn't simply told to be brave in a vacuum, but to lead a people already given God's law, meaning the "promise" of the land comes bound together with the responsibility to live according to God's commandments once they arrive. Courage without moral direction could become recklessness; God's law gives shape and purpose to the courage Joshua is called to display.

Explain the difference between "fact" and "opinion," and give ONE example of each about a topic of your choice. 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Fact: a statement that can be proven true or false with evidence. Example: "The Great Wall of China is over 13,000 miles long." Opinion: a personal belief or judgement that cannot be objectively proven. Example: "The Great Wall of China is the most impressive historical monument in the world."
Week 8 — Informative Writing
📝 Week 8 — Informative/Expository Writing
Week 8
2
Explain the PURPOSE of informative (expository) writing, and how it differs from persuasive writing. 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Informative (expository) writing aims to EXPLAIN or INFORM the reader about a topic clearly and objectively, without trying to change their opinion. This differs from persuasive writing, whose purpose is to CONVINCE the reader to agree with a particular viewpoint or take a specific action — informative writing presents balanced information, while persuasive writing argues for one side.
3
Write a short informative paragraph (at least 4 sentences) explaining how volcanoes form, using clear, objective language (no persuasive devices). 6 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Award marks for: clear, factual, objective tone without persuasive language; a logical explanation sequence; and accurate content. Example: "Volcanoes form when molten rock, known as magma, rises from deep within the Earth's crust. Pressure builds beneath the surface until it forces its way through weak points, erupting as lava, ash, or gas. Over time, repeated eruptions build up layers of cooled material, forming the cone-shaped mountain typically associated with volcanoes. Not all volcanoes are equally active — some remain dormant for centuries between eruptions."

📚 Week 7 — States of Matter
Week 7
1
Name the three main states of matter, and describe how particle arrangement differs between them. 5 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Solid: particles are tightly packed in a fixed, regular arrangement, only vibrating in place — giving solids a fixed shape and volume. Liquid: particles are close together but can move past one another, giving liquids a fixed volume but a shape that adapts to their container. Gas: particles are far apart and move freely and rapidly in all directions, so gases have neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.

Name the correct separation technique for each mixture, and briefly explain how it works: 6 marks

a) Sand mixed with water
b) Salt dissolved in water
c) A mixture of iron filings and sulphur powder
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
a) Filtration — the water passes through filter paper while the insoluble sand is trapped. b) Evaporation (or distillation to recover the water too) — heating causes the water to evaporate, leaving the salt behind. c) Magnetic separation — a magnet attracts and removes the iron filings, leaving the sulphur powder behind, since iron is magnetic and sulphur is not.
Week 9 — Elements, Compounds & the Periodic Table
🧬 Week 9 — Elements, Compounds & the Periodic Table
Week 9
3
Define "element" and "compound," and explain the key difference between them, using one example of each. 6 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Element: a pure substance made of only ONE type of atom, which cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means. Example: oxygen (O), iron (Fe). Compound: a substance formed when TWO or more different elements chemically combine in fixed proportions. Example: water (H₂O), carbon dioxide (CO₂). Key difference: elements contain only one type of atom, while compounds contain atoms of different elements chemically bonded together, with new properties different from the original elements.
Synthesis
🏆 Extended Synthesis Question
4
A student has a mixture containing salt, sand, and iron filings, dissolved/mixed together in water. Design a step-by-step method, using at least THREE different separation techniques (Week 8), to separate all three substances. Explain how understanding states of matter (Week 7) and the difference between elements/compounds/mixtures (Week 9) helps you plan this method. 8 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Step 1 — Magnetic separation: use a magnet to remove the iron filings first, since iron is magnetic and the other substances are not. Step 2 — Filtration: filter the remaining mixture to separate the insoluble sand from the salt water solution. Step 3 — Evaporation: heat the remaining salt water solution to evaporate the water, leaving solid salt crystals behind. Explanation: understanding that water is a liquid that changes state to a gas when heated (Week 7) explains WHY evaporation works to recover the salt. Understanding that salt is a compound that dissolves (its ions separate and mix at a particle level with water) while sand does not dissolve, and iron is an element with magnetic properties unlike the others, helps decide the correct ORDER and METHOD for separation, since a technique that works for one substance (like filtration for insoluble sand) would not work for the dissolved salt.

📚 Week 7 — Climate & Biomes
Week 7
1
Define "biome," and name TWO different biomes, briefly describing their climate. 5 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
A biome is a large region of the world characterised by a particular climate and the types of plants and animals adapted to live there. Accept any two of: Tropical rainforest — hot and very wet year-round. Desert — extremely low rainfall, often hot (though not always). Tundra — extremely cold, low precipitation, short growing season. Grassland/savanna — moderate rainfall with a distinct dry season.

💡 Show Model Answer ▼

Week 9 — Medieval Europe & the Feudal System
🏰 Week 9 — Medieval Europe & the Feudal System
Week 9
3
Describe the structure of the feudal system, naming at least THREE levels/roles in the correct hierarchical order. 6 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
The feudal system was structured (from top to bottom) as: Monarch (King/Queen) — owned all land and granted it to nobles in exchange for loyalty and military support. Nobles/Lords — managed large estates granted by the monarch, offering protection in exchange for service. Knights — provided military service to lords in exchange for land. Peasants/Serfs — worked the land at the bottom of the hierarchy, often with very limited freedom, in exchange for protection and the right to farm small plots.
Synthesis
🏆 Extended Synthesis Question
4
Explain how physical geography (climate/biomes, Week 7, and settlement factors, Week 8) shaped WHERE medieval European settlements and feudal estates (Week 9) were typically located and how successful they could become. Write at least 6 sentences. 8 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Medieval European settlements and feudal estates were strongly shaped by the same physical geography factors studied in Weeks 7 and 8. Fertile land and reliable water access (Week 8's settlement factors) determined which manors and estates could support larger populations and generate greater agricultural surplus, directly affecting a lord's wealth and the size of the peasant population working the land. Climate and biome (Week 7) mattered enormously too: estates located in temperate biomes with predictable rainfall could grow reliable harvests year after year, supporting stable feudal relationships, while regions with harsher climates (closer to tundra-like conditions, or areas prone to drought) struggled to support the same density of population or produce reliable surplus for lords to extract as tax or tribute. This meant that the most powerful and stable feudal estates tended to be concentrated in regions with the most favourable physical geography — good soil, reliable water, and moderate climate — showing that the social and political structure of the feudal system (Week 9) was ultimately built upon, and constrained by, the physical geography beneath it (Weeks 7-8).
