📖 Joseph — Character Through Suffering

Genesis 37, 39–45 · Dreams, Betrayal & Forgiveness · God's Purposes in Difficult Circumstances
Genesis 50:20Genesis 37–45ForgivenessKS3 Level 6

Memory Verse

"You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good, to accomplish what is now being done, the saving of many lives."
Genesis 50:20 — NIV
Word-by-word: "You intended to harm me" = Joseph names the wrong done to him honestly. "God intended it for good" = the SAME events can carry two intentions at once. "the saving of many lives" = the good that came was not small — it rescued an entire region from famine.

A Family Torn by Favouritism — Genesis 37

Joseph was the eleventh of Jacob's twelve sons but the first son of his beloved wife Rachel. Jacob's elaborate robe for Joseph — plus Joseph's two dreams of his family bowing to him — fuelled his brothers' resentment.

What Fuelled the Hatred

  • Jacob's open, unequal favouritism
  • The expensive, symbolic robe
  • Joseph reporting brothers' misdeeds
  • Joseph's two dreams of ruling over them

How Far It Went

  • "They hated him... could not speak a kind word" (37:4)
  • Some brothers wanted to kill him (37:20)
  • Reuben persuades them to use a pit instead
  • Judah suggests selling him for silver (37:26-27)
KS3 thinking point: Joseph was about 17 (37:2) when betrayed. Unlike Noah or Abraham, his suffering begins through other PEOPLE'S choices, not a direct call from God.

Joseph's Two Dreams — Genesis 37:5-11

Dream One + Dream Two

Dream One: Sheaves of wheat — Joseph's sheaf stands upright; his brothers' sheaves bow down to it.

Dream Two: The sun, moon, and eleven stars bow down to Joseph — even Jacob is troubled.

KS3 key idea: Both dreams come true literally — but not for over twenty years, and not before unimaginable suffering. God's promises can be true long before they look true.

From the Pit to the Palace — Joseph's Long Road

1
Sold into Slavery (Gen 37): Brothers throw Joseph in a pit, sell him for silver; he is taken to Egypt and sold to Potiphar.
2
Falsely Accused & Imprisoned (Gen 39): Serves Potiphar with integrity, but is falsely accused by Potiphar's wife and imprisoned.
3
Interpreting Dreams in Prison (Gen 40-41): Correctly interprets two officials' dreams; later remembered and brought before Pharaoh.
4
Second-in-Command of Egypt (Gen 41): Interprets Pharaoh's dreams as 7 years plenty then 7 years famine; made ruler over Egypt at about age 30.
5
Reunion & Forgiveness (Gen 42-45): Brothers travel to Egypt for grain and bow before the brother they sold; Joseph forgives them.
Timeline link: About 13 years pass between Joseph being sold (age 17) and standing before Pharaoh (age 30) — over two decades before the dreams of Genesis 37 are fulfilled.

The Moment of Forgiveness — Genesis 45:4-8

Brothers ExpectedWhat Joseph Actually Said
Revenge or punishment"I am Joseph your brother, whom you sold into Egypt!" (45:4)
Permanent rejection"Do not be distressed and do not be angry with yourselves" (45:5)

ESL note: Forgiveness does NOT mean pretending the wrong didn't happen (Joseph names it plainly). It means choosing not to let that wrong control how you treat the person now.

Comparing Three Examples of Faith

FeatureNoah (Wk4)Abraham (Wk5)Joseph (Wk6)
Source of hardshipNatural disasterHis own choice to obeyOther people's cruelty

The pattern deepens: Joseph's story is the first where the difficulty comes from other people's SIN rather than God's instruction or a disaster — yet God's purpose still moves forward.

Building Academic Vocabulary — Step by Step

1
Break the word into parts: "in-flex-ible" = not + bend + able to be.
2
Guess the meaning from the parts: "not able to be bent."
3
Check the word in context — confirm your guess fits the sentence.
4
Collect the word family: flex, flexible, inflexible, flexibility, flexibly.
5
Use the word actively in your own sentence the same day.

Converting Fractions to Decimals — Divide!

3/8 → decimal

3/8 means 3 ÷ 8

3 ÷ 8 = 0.375

Golden rule: a fraction is just a division — always divide numerator by denominator.

Converting Decimals to Percentages — ×100

Decimal → Percentage

0.375 × 100 = 37.5 → write as 37.5%

0.6 × 100 = 60 → write as 60%

Reverse: Percentage → Decimal: divide by 100. 82% ÷ 100 = 0.82

Common mistake: multiplying by 100 just moves the decimal point two places right — it does not add random zeros.

Finding a Percentage of an Amount

1
Method A — Decimal multiplier: 30% of 80 → 0.30 × 80 = 24.
2
Method B — Find 1%, then scale: 1% of 80 = 0.8, so 30% = 0.8 × 30 = 24.
3
Mental shortcut — 10% and 50%: 10% of 80 = 8, 5% = 4, so 15% = 8 + 4 = 12.
Worked example: Find 65% of 220

Decimal multiplier: 0.65 × 220 = 143

Check: 60% = 132, 5% = 11, 60%+5% = 143 ✓

Percentage Increase & Decrease — Real-Life Maths

ScenarioWorkingAnswer
£40 jacket, 25% discount25% of 40 = 10. £40 − £10£30
Population of 5,000 grows 12%12% of 5,000 = 600. 5,000 + 6005,600
£15 up 20%, then down 20%£15 → £18 → −£3.60£14.40, not £15!
KS3 key idea: A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does NOT return the original amount — the second 20% is calculated on a different (larger) number. A common exam trap!

Producers, Consumers & Decomposers

RoleHow It Gets EnergyExamples
ProducerPhotosynthesis (Week 5 link!)Grass, trees, algae

KS3 key idea: Without decomposers, nutrients stay locked in dead material forever — they recycle the building blocks of life back into the soil.

Building a Food Chain

Woodland Food Chain

🌾 Grass → 🐇 Rabbit → 🦊 Fox → 🍄 Fungi (decomposer)

Arrow direction: Arrows show the direction energy FLOWS — from the organism eaten TO the one eating it. "Grass → Rabbit" means rabbit eats grass, not the other way around.

Food Webs — More Realistic Than a Single Chain

Most animals eat — and are eaten by — MORE THAN ONE species. A food web links many overlapping chains together. Example: in a woodland, both foxes and owls eat mice AND rabbits — they share prey, so they are part of the same web.

Golden idea: Removing ONE organism from a food web can affect many others throughout the whole system. This is why biodiversity is so important — more links mean a more stable web.

Energy Pyramids — Why Food Chains Are Short

KS3 key idea: Only ~10% of energy passes on at each level (the rest is lost as heat). This is why one field can have thousands of grass plants, hundreds of rabbits, but only a few foxes.

How Rivers Shape the Land

1
Source: A river begins at a spring, glacier, or high-altitude lake.
2
Upper Course: Fast, narrow; cuts steep valleys through higher ground.
3
Middle & Lower Course: Widens and slows; forms large meanders; deposits fertile soil.
4
Mouth (Delta/Estuary): Reaches the sea, sometimes splitting into a fan-shaped delta.
KS3 key idea: Many early civilisations grew up along major rivers (Nile, Tigris-Euphrates, Indus, Yellow River) because of fertile river-deposited soil.

Two Contrasting Regions

🏜️ Sahara Desert

  • Largest HOT desert on Earth
  • North Africa — spans ~9.2 million km²
  • Extremely low rainfall; very sparse vegetation
  • Very hot days; near-freezing nights

🌳 Amazon Rainforest

  • Largest tropical rainforest on Earth
  • South America, mostly Brazil
  • Very high rainfall; richest biodiversity
  • Hot and humid year-round
KS3 key idea: "Desert" means VERY LOW RAINFALL, not necessarily heat — Antarctica is the world's largest desert overall. You will explore climate zones and biomes further in Week 7.
