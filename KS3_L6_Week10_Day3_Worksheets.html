🔍 Structured Paragraphs
2
Write a structured paragraph explaining why David's story is UNIQUE among the figures studied so far, because it is the first major story where the central figure's greatest failure comes from THEMSELVES rather than external circumstances or other people. Use specific evidence. 5 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
David's story occupies a distinctive place among the figures of faith studied this term because it is the first major narrative where the central character's most serious failure originates from their OWN choices, rather than external circumstance or the wrongdoing of others. Noah faced a natural disaster; Abraham faced the challenge of an unclear destination he did not choose to make difficult; Joseph's suffering came entirely from his brothers' cruelty and Potiphar's wife's false accusation; Joshua's challenge was military conquest of an external enemy. David, by contrast, was not the victim of anyone else's wrongdoing when he took Bathsheba and arranged Uriah's death — the sin originated entirely from his own desire and abuse of royal power. This is significant because it shifts the theological question from "how does a person of faith respond to difficulty imposed from outside?" to a harder question: "how does a person of faith respond when THEY THEMSELVES are the source of the difficulty?" David's honest repentance in this context — rather than external endurance or courage — becomes the model of faithfulness his story offers, making it a necessary complement to the other figures' stories, since every person will, at some point, need a model for facing their OWN failure, not just external hardship.

2
Each answer contains one error. Find it, explain it, and give the correct answer: 6 marks

a) "Reflecting (3, 8) over the x-axis gives (−3, 8)"

🏆 Extended Scientific Evaluation
3
"Understanding the difference between physical and chemical change is essential for making sense of nearly everything studied in science this term." Evaluate this statement, connecting to AT LEAST TWO other science topics studied previously (Week 8 — mixtures and separation, or Week 9 — elements and the periodic table). Write at least 8 sentences. 10 marks
🔗 Cross-week connections available: Week 8 Science — mixtures, separation techniques. Week 9 Science — elements, compounds, periodic table.
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
This statement is strongly supported by the way this term's science topics build directly upon one another around the central physical/chemical distinction. In Week 8, the entire logic of separation techniques (filtration, evaporation, distillation, chromatography) depends on understanding that MIXTURES can be separated because their components remain PHYSICALLY combined without any chemical reaction — each substance retains its own original chemical identity, which is precisely why physical methods (based on particle size, boiling point, solubility) can successfully pull them apart again. Without understanding the physical/chemical distinction, a student could not understand WHY these separation techniques work in the first place — they work specifically because no chemical bonding has occurred. In Week 9, the study of elements and compounds directly extends this distinction: a compound (like sodium chloride) is fundamentally different from a mixture precisely because it has undergone a CHEMICAL change — elements have chemically bonded in a fixed ratio to create an entirely new substance with new properties (safe table salt from dangerous sodium and chlorine), which cannot be separated by the simple physical techniques used for mixtures. This week's lesson on physical versus chemical changes therefore provides the essential conceptual foundation that makes sense of both previous weeks: mixtures involve only physical combination (reversible, separable by physical means), while compounds involve genuine chemical change (irreversible without further chemical reactions, requiring entirely different methods to break apart, such as electrolysis). Without a clear grasp of what distinguishes a physical from a chemical change, a student would be unable to fully understand WHY mixtures behave differently from compounds, or why certain separation techniques work for one but not the other — making this week's topic not an isolated unit but a genuine unifying principle running through the term's chemistry content.

🔍 Comparative Analysis
2
Compare the Crusades to another period of significant cultural contact between civilisations you have studied this term — for example, ancient trade routes, or the spread of ideas via the Roman Empire (Week 4). What is SIMILAR about how cultural exchange happens (regardless of whether the initial contact was peaceful or violent), and what makes the Crusades' case distinctive? 8 marks
🔗 Cross-week connection: Week 4 History — the Roman Empire and how it spread ideas, technology, and culture across its territories.
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Comparison — the Roman Empire (Week 4): The Roman Empire spread technology, engineering knowledge (roads, aqueducts), legal systems, and Latin language across its vast territory largely through deliberate governance, administration, and settlement rather than primarily through warfare with equal or rival civilisations — though initial conquest was often violent, the LONG-TERM cultural exchange occurred through sustained political integration and administration within a single unified empire. What is similar: In both cases, extended CONTACT between different peoples — whether through empire, trade, or war — created channels through which ideas, technology, and knowledge could transfer, regardless of the ORIGINAL motivation for contact. Neither Rome's expansion nor the Crusades were initially motivated by a desire for cultural or scientific exchange (Rome sought territorial control and tribute; the Crusaders sought religious/military goals) — yet significant, lasting cultural transfer occurred as a SECONDARY consequence of the contact itself. What makes the Crusades distinctive: Unlike the Roman Empire, which integrated conquered peoples into a single unified political and administrative system over centuries, the Crusades involved contact between two civilisations that remained politically SEPARATE and largely opposed throughout — Christian Europe and the Islamic world never merged into one empire. This means the cultural exchange occurred through more fragmented, indirect channels (trade, translated texts, scholarly contact in border regions like Spain and Sicily) rather than through direct governmental integration, making the Crusades a distinctive case of significant cultural transfer occurring WITHOUT political unification — knowledge crossed a genuine civilisational and religious divide that remained largely intact even as ideas flowed across it.
3
Connect this week's History to this week's Maths: coordinates use a system of precise LOCATION (x, y) to describe exact positions. Explain how the Crusades depended on medieval understanding of GEOGRAPHIC location and navigation to move armies across vast distances — what geographic/navigational knowledge would have been essential, and how might improved navigational tools (like the astrolabe, mentioned in the lesson) have specifically helped? 6 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Essential geographic/navigational knowledge: Moving thousands of knights, soldiers, and supplies across enormous distances — from Western Europe to the Eastern Mediterranean, either by extremely long overland routes through unfamiliar and often hostile territory, or by sea across the Mediterranean — required substantial geographic knowledge: reliable maps or route knowledge, understanding of seasonal weather patterns for safe sea travel, knowledge of where supplies, water, and safe passage could be found along the route, and the ability to determine position and direction accurately, especially at sea where there were no landmarks to navigate by. How the astrolabe helped: The astrolabe — an instrument that could determine one's position and the time by measuring the angle of stars or the sun above the horizon — allowed sailors and travellers to calculate their approximate latitude even when far from any visible landmark, which was crucial for the long sea voyages Crusaders undertook across the Mediterranean. Before more advanced navigational tools became widely available in Europe (partly through this very cultural exchange with the Islamic world, where the astrolabe had been significantly refined), sea travel was considerably riskier and less precise, making it harder to reliably reach a specific destination or return safely — the adoption of tools like the astrolabe therefore directly improved the practical logistics of the sustained, long-distance military campaigns that the Crusades required.
