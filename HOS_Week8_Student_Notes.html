📖 The Ten Commandments

Exodus 20 · God's Law for Daily Life · Loving God and Loving Others
Exodus 20Matthew 22:37-39God's LawKS3 Level 6

Memory Verse

"Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind… Love your neighbour as yourself."
Matthew 22:37,39 — NIV
What this means: Jesus is asked "which commandment is greatest?" He does not pick one of the ten — he gives TWO that summarise ALL ten. Commandments 1-4 = loving God. Commandments 5-10 = loving people.

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample / Note
commandmentA strong law or rule given by God — not just advice.There are 10 commandments in Exodus 20.
covenantA serious, binding agreement between God and people.The Ten Commandments are part of God's covenant with Israel.
idolatryWorshipping an idol (statue or image) instead of the real God.Commandment 2 bans idolatry.
sabbathThe seventh day, set aside as a rest day by God's command.God rested on day 7 of creation (Genesis 2:2).
honourTo show deep respect and value to someone.Commandment 5: honour your father and mother.
covetTo want what belongs to someone else in a jealous, unhealthy way.Commandment 10 — the only command about a thought, not an action.
bear false witnessTo lie about someone, especially in court.Commandment 9 protects people's reputations.
moral lawRules about right and wrong that come from God's own character.The Ten Commandments are moral law — timeless, not just for one culture.

Why Were the Commandments Given? — Exodus 20:1-2

Before God gives any commands, he says: "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt, out of the land of slavery." This is very important. God saved Israel FIRST — then gave the law as a guide for living as a rescued people. The law is not a way to EARN God's love; it is a gift to people who already have it.

Key point: Grace (rescue) always comes BEFORE law (guidance) in the Bible. God saved first; then said "now here is how to live well."

The Ten Commandments — All Ten Explained

Commandment 1
No other gods
God alone is Lord. Nothing else should take his place in our lives — not money, fame, or any other thing.
Commandment 2
No idols
Do not worship images. God cannot be reduced to a statue made by human hands.
Commandment 3
Honour God's name
Do not use God's name carelessly or disrespectfully. His name represents who he is.
Commandment 4
Keep the Sabbath
Rest one day in seven. God built rest into creation. This protects people from becoming slaves to work.
Commandment 5
Honour parents
Treat parents with love and respect. The only commandment with a specific blessing promised.
Commandment 6
Do not murder
Human life is valuable because people are made in God's image. Unjustly taking life destroys what God values.
Commandment 7
Do not commit adultery
Marriage is a covenant — a sacred promise. Breaking it causes deep harm to people.
Commandment 8
Do not steal
What belongs to others should be respected. Stealing violates both their rights and your own integrity.
Commandment 9
Do not lie about others
Truth builds community. Lying about someone destroys their reputation and breaks social trust.
Commandment 10
Do not covet
The only commandment about a desire, not an action. God cares about our hearts, not just our behaviour.
Two categories: Commandments 1-4 = your relationship with GOD (vertical). Commandments 5-10 = your relationships with PEOPLE (horizontal). Both matter equally.

✏️ Informative & Expository Writing

How to Organise Ideas · Introduction · Body · Conclusion · Topic Sentences · Linking Words
Essay StructureTopic SentencesCohesive DevicesKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
expositoryWriting that explains or gives information clearly, without personal opinion.A science report or history essay is expository writing.
thesis statement1-2 sentences at the end of the introduction saying exactly what the essay will explain."This essay will show how rivers shaped ancient civilisations."
topic sentenceThe first sentence of a body paragraph — states what that one paragraph is about."The Nile provided Egypt with fertile farmland."
supporting detailFacts, evidence, or examples that prove the topic sentence.Statistics, historical facts, quotes from the lesson.
cohesive deviceWords that connect sentences and paragraphs smoothly.Furthermore, However, As a result, For example.
conclusionThe final paragraph — summarises main points and gives a final thought. No new ideas."In conclusion, the commandments shaped both personal and community life."
formal registerAcademic, professional writing style — no slang, no contractions, full sentences.Write "do not" not "don't"; "however" not "but".

The Three-Part Structure

1
Introduction: Hook (interesting first sentence) + background (2-3 sentences of context) + thesis statement (what your essay will explain).
2
Body Paragraphs: One idea per paragraph. Each paragraph = topic sentence → supporting detail 1 → supporting detail 2 → supporting detail 3 → linking sentence. 2-4 body paragraphs for KS3.
3
Conclusion: Restate thesis in NEW words. Briefly summarise key points. Add a final thought — why does this matter? Never introduce new ideas in the conclusion.
Remember: Introduction = the door. Body paragraphs = the rooms (one idea each). Conclusion = the exit. Your reader should know exactly what to expect from each section.

Cohesive Devices — Connecting Your Writing

PurposeWords to Use
Adding informationFurthermore, In addition, Moreover, Also
Showing contrastHowever, On the other hand, In contrast, Nevertheless
Showing cause / effectAs a result, Therefore, Consequently, This means that
Giving examplesFor example, For instance, Such as, Including
ConcludingIn conclusion, To summarise, Overall, In short

Strong vs Weak Topic Sentences

❌ Weak✅ Strong
"This paragraph is about rivers.""The Nile River made early Egyptian civilisation possible by providing fertile farmland."
"Pollution is bad.""Air pollution from factories has significantly increased respiratory illness in urban areas."
"The commandments are important.""Commandments 5-10 provided ancient Israel with a complete framework for social order."
Formula: Strong topic sentence = specific SUBJECT + clear CLAIM about it. The claim should be something you can prove with evidence.

🧮 Introduction to Algebra

What Algebra Is · Writing Expressions · Collecting Like Terms · Substitution
ExpressionsLike TermsSubstitutionKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
algebraA branch of maths using letters to represent unknown numbers.From Arabic "al-jabr" = reunion of broken parts.
variableA letter that represents an unknown number.x, y, n, a, b are all variables.
termA single number, variable, or combination like 3x.In 4x + 7, there are two terms: 4x and 7.
expressionA group of terms — NO equals sign.3x + 2y − 5 is an expression.
coefficientThe number in front of a variable.In 7y, the coefficient is 7.
like termsTerms with the SAME variable. Only like terms can be added/subtracted.3x and 5x are like terms. 3x and 3y are NOT.
simplifyTo shorten an expression by collecting like terms.3x + 5x simplifies to 8x.
substitutionReplacing a variable with a number to find a value.If x = 4, then 3x + 2 = 14.

Writing Algebraic Expressions

Key rules: Never write × between a number and a letter — write 5n not 5×n. Coefficient comes first: 3y not y3. If the coefficient is 1, just write the letter: 1x = x.
In WordsExpression
A number n plus 7n + 7
Three times a number x3x
A number y divided by 4y ÷ 4 (or y/4)
5 less than twice a number2n − 5
The sum of two unknownsx + y

Collecting Like Terms — Worked Examples

3x + 5x = 8x    ← same variable x; add coefficients: 3+5=8
7y − 3y = 4y    ← same variable y; 7−3=4
4a + 3b + 2a − b = 6a + 2b   ← group a-terms, then b-terms
5x + 3 − 2x + 7 = 3x + 10   ← numbers without variables are also like terms
Common mistake: 3x + 5 CANNOT be simplified — x and a plain number are NOT like terms. Leave it as 3x + 5.

Substitution

Find 3x + 5 when x = 4:   3(4) + 5 = 12 + 5 = 17
Find 2a − b when a = 6, b = 3:   2(6) − 3 = 12 − 3 = 9
Find x² + 2x when x = 3:   (3)² + 2(3) = 9 + 6 = 15
Always use brackets when you substitute a number — it prevents mistakes with negative numbers and multiplication.

🔬 Mixtures & Separation Techniques

Filtration · Evaporation · Distillation · Chromatography · Pure vs Mixed Substances
ChemistryFiltrationDistillationKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
mixtureTwo or more substances mixed together but NOT chemically joined.Salt water, muddy water, ink, air.
pure substanceOnly one type of substance — always same properties.Pure water, pure gold, pure salt.
solubleAble to dissolve in a liquid.Salt is soluble in water; sand is insoluble.
filtrationUsing a filter to separate an insoluble solid from a liquid.Sand from water.
evaporationHeating a solution so liquid escapes as gas — solid stays behind.Getting salt from salt water.
distillationBoiling a liquid, collecting the vapour, cooling it back — getting pure liquid.Getting pure water from salt water.
chromatographySeparating coloured substances by speed they travel through paper.Separating dyes in ink or food colouring.
solute / solvent / solutionSolute = what dissolves. Solvent = liquid it dissolves in. Solution = the result.Salt (solute) + water (solvent) = salt water (solution).

Mixtures vs Pure Substances

Mixture

  • Two or more substances together
  • Each keeps its own properties
  • No chemical reaction
  • Can be separated physically
  • Examples: air, ink, sea water

Pure Substance

  • Only ONE type of particle
  • Fixed melting and boiling point
  • Cannot be separated further (physically)
  • Always the same properties
  • Examples: pure water, pure gold

The Four Separation Techniques

1
Filtration: Pour mixture through filter paper. Liquid (filtrate) passes through — insoluble solid (residue) stays behind. Use for: muddy water, sand + water.
2
Evaporation: Heat a solution in an evaporating dish. Water turns to steam and escapes. Solid (e.g. salt) is left as crystals. Use when: you want the SOLID.
3
Distillation: Heat a solution — liquid boils first. Collect the vapour in a condenser (cool tube) — vapour cools back to liquid. Use when: you want the LIQUID (pure water).
4
Chromatography: Spot of ink/dye on paper. Place paper in solvent. Solvent moves up, carrying dyes at different speeds — dyes separate into coloured bands. Use for: identifying unknown dyes.
Key idea: All techniques use PHYSICAL properties — not chemical reactions. The substances are sorted, not changed.

Choosing the Right Technique

MixtureTechnique
Sand + water (sand does NOT dissolve)Filtration
Salt water — want the SALTEvaporation
Salt water — want PURE WATERDistillation
Ink made of several dyesChromatography
Sand + salt + water (all three)Filtration first, then evaporation

🏛️ Population & Settlement

Why People Settle · Rural vs Urban · Push and Pull Factors · Migration · Urbanisation
SettlementPush & PullUrbanisationKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
settlementA place where people live permanently — from a tiny village to a huge city.Ancient Egypt settled along the Nile.
population densityHow many people live in a given area (people per km²).Tokyo = very high density. Sahara Desert = very low density.
ruralCountryside areas — farmland, forests, small villages. Low density.Farming communities, small villages.
urbanCity and town areas. High population density, many services.London, New York, Karachi.
urbanisationThe process of more people moving from countryside to cities — a global trend.In 1800: 3% urban. Today: over 55% urban.
migrationMovement of people from one place to another to live.Internal = within one country. International = between countries.
push factorsReasons that push people AWAY from where they live.War, drought, poverty, lack of jobs.
pull factorsReasons that pull people TOWARDS a new place.Better jobs, safety, education, family.

Why People Settle Where They Do

FactorWhy It MattersHistorical Example
Water supplyEvery settlement needs fresh water. Rivers and springs were the first reason people stopped moving.All earliest cities were on rivers: Nile, Tigris-Euphrates, Indus.
Fertile landFlat, rich soil allows farming and feeding large numbers of people.Nile River valley — annual floods deposited rich silt.
Natural defenceHills, cliffs, rivers, and coasts protected against attack.Medieval towns built on hills or surrounded by water.
Mild climateModerate temperature and rainfall make life easier.Extreme places (deserts, polar) have very low population density.
Transport routesPorts, river crossings, and road junctions brought trade and wealth.London grew because of its Thames river port.
Natural resourcesCoal, forests, fish — places with resources attracted workers.Cities grew near UK coalfields in the Industrial Revolution.

Push and Pull Factors

⬅️ Push Factors (reasons to LEAVE)

  • War and conflict
  • Poverty and unemployment
  • Drought and famine
  • Lack of schools or hospitals
  • Political persecution

➡️ Pull Factors (reasons to GO THERE)

  • Better job opportunities
  • Safety and political freedom
  • Good schools and universities
  • Joining family members
  • A more comfortable climate
Remember: Refugees are pushed out by danger. Economic migrants are pulled by opportunity. Both are migration — but for different reasons.
