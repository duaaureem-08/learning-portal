Memory Verse

"Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, 'Whom shall I send? And who will go for us?' And I said, 'Here am I. Send me!'"
Isaiah 6:8 — NIV
What this means: This is Isaiah's response right after seeing God's holiness and being cleansed of his sin — immediate and willing, not reluctant.

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample / Note

idolatryWorshipping a false god instead of the true God.Elijah confronted worship of Baal.
repentanceGenuinely turning from wrongdoing back to God.A central prophetic message.
visionA powerful, symbolic experience revealing something from God.Isaiah's vision of God in the temple.
holinessBeing set apart, pure, completely different from ordinary/sinful things."Holy, holy, holy" — Isaiah 6:3.
exileBeing forced to leave one's home country.Isaiah's prophecies address coming exile.
MessiahA promised future rescuer/king.Isaiah gives clear Old Testament descriptions of the Messiah.

Why Did God Send Prophets?

PurposeExplanation
Call people back to GodConfronted idolatry and injustice, called for repentance.
Warn of consequencesWarned that continued disobedience would bring real consequences.
Offer hopeIncluded restoration and hope — judgment was never the final word.
Speak God's message directly"This is what the LORD says" — not their own opinion.

Elijah and the Prophets of Baal — 1 Kings 18

1
The challenge: "How long will you waver between two opinions?" (18:21)
2
The contest: Baal's prophets cry out for hours — nothing. Elijah prays briefly — fire falls instantly.
3
Response: "The LORD — he is God!" (18:39)

Elijah's Despair and God's Gentle Whisper — 1 Kings 19

Immediately after victory, Elijah flees in fear and despair (19:4). God's response: rest and food first, then "a gentle whisper" (19:12) — not more spectacle.

Key idea: Even faithful, powerful people can experience real burnout — God's response models compassion, not just demands.

Isaiah's Vision and Calling — Isaiah 6

1
Isaiah sees God's holiness — "Holy, holy, holy" (6:3).
2
Isaiah recognises his unworthiness — "Woe to me!" (6:5).
3
Isaiah is cleansed — sin "atoned for" (6:7).
4
Isaiah volunteers — "Here am I. Send me!" (6:8).
Key idea: Willingness to be sent comes AFTER encounter and cleansing, not before.

✏️ Pronoun Use & Agreement

Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement · Avoiding Vague Pronoun Reference
PronounsAntecedentsKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
pronounA word that replaces a noun to avoid repeating it.he, she, it, they, this, those.
antecedentThe noun a pronoun refers back to."Sara lost her keys" — Sara is the antecedent.
agreementA pronoun must match its antecedent in number/gender."The dog wagged its tail."
vague referenceWhen a pronoun's antecedent is unclear."When Sara spoke to Amy, she seemed upset" — who?
indefinite pronounDoesn't refer to one specific thing.everyone, someone, each — mostly singular.

Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement

Golden rule: A pronoun must match its antecedent in NUMBER and GENDER. Singular antecedent → singular pronoun. Plural antecedent → plural pronoun.

❌ Incorrect✅ Correct
"Neither of the boys brought their homework.""Neither of the boys brought his homework." ("Neither" is singular.)
Inconsistent team agreement"The team celebrated its win" (UK formal) — be consistent.

Vague Pronoun Reference

❌ Vague✅ Clear
"When Sara spoke to Amy, she seemed upset.""When Sara spoke to Amy, Amy seemed upset."
"I love reading and writing, which is my favourite subject.""I love reading and writing; English is my favourite subject."
"They say it's going to rain.""The weather forecaster says it's going to rain."

Finding the Antecedent — Method

1
Identify the pronoun.
2
Look BACKWARD for the noun it refers to.
3
Check for more than one possible match (= vague reference problem).
4
Check agreement (number/gender).

Practice Questions

🧮 Negative Numbers

negative numberA number less than zero.−5

absolute valueDistance a number is from zero, always positive.|−7| = 7

Ordering Negative Numbers

Golden rule: Numbers get LARGER moving RIGHT on the number line. −2 is GREATER than −5 (further right).

Common mistake: −8 is NOT bigger than −3 — the opposite is true: −3 > −8 (closer to zero).

Adding and Subtracting Negatives

Adding a negative = subtracting: 5+(−3) = 5−3 = 2
Subtracting a negative = adding: 5−(−3) = 5+3 = 8
Negative + positive: −4+6 = 2
Negative − more: −4−3 = −7
Memory trick: "Two minuses make a plus" — rewrite double signs first.

Multiplying/Dividing with Negatives

SignsResultExample

Positive × NegativeNegative3×(−4)=−12
Negative × NegativePositive(−3)×(−4)=12

Real-Life Contexts

1
Temperature: −5°C means 5 degrees below freezing.
2
Bank balances: −£20 means you owe £20.
3
Elevation: −400m means 400m below sea level.

The Age of Exploration

By the 1400s overland routes became harder/costlier — especially after Constantinople fell to the Ottomans (1453) — pushing Europeans to seek sea routes.
