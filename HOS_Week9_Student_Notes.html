📖 Joshua — Courage & Promise

Joshua 1, 3-6 · Entering the Promised Land · God Keeps His Promises
Joshua 1:9Joshua 3-6CourageKS3 Level 6

Memory Verse

"Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged, for the LORD your God will be with you wherever you go."
Joshua 1:9 — NIV
What this means: This command is repeated FOUR times in Joshua 1. "Be strong and courageous" does not mean fear disappears — it means fear should not control your decisions, because God's presence goes with you everywhere.

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample / Note
successorThe person who takes over a role after someone else.Joshua became Moses' successor.
Promised LandThe land of Canaan, promised by God to Abraham's descendants.Israel waited generations to enter it.
covenantA serious, binding agreement between God and his people.The land was part of God's covenant with Abraham.
ark of the covenantA sacred chest representing God's presence with Israel.Carried by priests at the Jordan and Jericho.
spies / scoutsPeople sent secretly to gather information before an army arrives.Joshua was one of 12 spies sent into Canaan (Numbers 13).
siegeSurrounding a city to force it to surrender.Jericho's "siege" was unusual — won by marching, not weapons.
consecrateTo set apart as holy or dedicated to God.Israel consecrated themselves before crossing the Jordan.
inheritanceSomething passed down or given as a lasting possession.Canaan was Israel's inheritance from God.

From Moses to Joshua

Moses led Israel out of Egypt and through 40 years in the wilderness but died before entering Canaan (Deuteronomy 34). Joshua — Moses' assistant and one of the original 12 spies — was chosen by God to lead Israel into the Promised Land.

Key point: God's first words to Joshua are a repeated command to be courageous, not a battle plan. Leadership transitions are tested moments — God meets uncertainty with the reassurance of his unchanging presence.

Crossing the Jordan — Joshua 3-4

1
Consecration: Israel prepares spiritually — "tomorrow the LORD will do amazing things among you."
2
Priests step in first: Carrying the ark, the priests step into the flooded river — the water stops flowing.
3
All Israel crosses: The whole nation crosses on dry ground while priests stand in the middle.
4
Twelve stones: One stone per tribe set up as a memorial for future generations to remember.
KS3 key idea: This crossing deliberately echoes the Red Sea crossing under Moses — the same God acts through Joshua.

The Battle of Jericho — Joshua 6

God's instructions: march around Jericho once a day for six days, then seven times on the seventh day, blow trumpets, and shout. No weapons. The walls collapsed (6:20).

KS3 key idea: Victory came through obedience to an unusual instruction, not military strength. The land was God's gift, not Israel's achievement.

Comparing Four Examples of Faith

FeatureAbraham (Wk5)Joseph (Wk6)Moses (Wk7)Joshua (Wk9)
What faith requiredLeaving home for an unknown landIntegrity through sufferingConfronting PharaohTaking a promise already given
Key command"Go" (Gen 12:1)(endurance)"Let my people go""Be strong and courageous"
The pattern completes: Joshua's story is the fulfilment of a promise made nearly 600 years earlier to Abraham — proof that God's promises take time but are never forgotten.

✏️ Speaking & Listening — Presentations

Structuring an Oral Presentation · Delivery Skills · Active Listening
Oral PresentationActive ListeningKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
presentationA talk given to inform, explain, or persuade an audience.Usually supported by slides or notes.
audienceThe people listening to a presentation.A good speaker thinks about what they already know.
active listeningFully concentrating on and engaging with a speaker.Shown through eye contact and relevant questions.
articulationSpeaking clearly so each word is understood.Not mumbling or rushing words together.
paceThe speed at which you speak.Too fast = hard to follow; too slow = loses interest.
eye contactLooking at the audience while speaking.Builds connection and trust.
filler wordsUnnecessary sounds when thinking, e.g. "um," "like."Reducing fillers sounds more confident.

Structuring a Presentation

1
Opening: Hook (question, fact, story) + clear statement of purpose: "Today I'm going to explain..."
2
Main points: 2-4 clear points, each signposted: "My first point is..." / "Next..." / "Finally..."
3
Closing: Summarise briefly + end strongly — a call to action or memorable final statement.
Key idea: Listeners cannot re-read a missed sentence — so structure and signposting matter even more in speech than in writing.

Delivery Skills

SkillWhat To DoMistake to Avoid
VolumeSpeak loud enough for everyone to hear.Speaking too quietly from nerves.
PaceMeasured pace; pause between points.Rushing through everything.
Eye contactLook at different parts of the audience.Reading only from notes.
Body languageStand confidently; natural gestures.Crossed arms, fidgeting.
Filler wordsPause silently instead.Overusing "um" / "like".

Active Listening

✅ Active Listening

  • Eye contact with the speaker
  • Nodding / facial reactions
  • Relevant follow-up questions
  • Waiting for pauses
  • Summarising to check understanding

❌ Poor Listening

  • Looking at a phone
  • Interrupting
  • Thinking of your own response, not theirs
  • Asking something already answered
  • Visible boredom

🧮 Solving One-Step Equations

The Balancing Method · Inverse Operations · Checking Answers
EquationsBalancing MethodKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
equationA statement that two things are EQUAL, using "=".x + 5 = 12
solveTo find the value of the unknown variable.Solve x + 5 = 12 → x = 7
balancing methodDoing the same operation to both sides so it stays equal.Like keeping a scale level.
inverse operationThe operation that "undoes" another.Inverse of + is −. Inverse of × is ÷.
one-step equationAn equation solved with just one inverse operation.x + 3 = 10 → subtract 3.
check / verifySubstituting your answer back to confirm it's correct.7+5=12 ✓

The Balancing Method

An equation is like a balanced scale. Whatever you do to ONE side, you must do EXACTLY the same to the OTHER side.

If the equation has...Use this inverseExample
x + 5 = 12Subtract 5 from both sidesx = 7
x − 4 = 9Add 4 to both sidesx = 13
3x = 21Divide both sides by 3x = 7
x ÷ 5 = 6Multiply both sides by 5x = 30

Worked Examples

Solve x + 8 = 15: x+8−8 = 15−8 → x = 7
Solve y − 6 = 11: y−6+6 = 11+6 → y = 17
Solve 4x = 28: 4x÷4 = 28÷4 → x = 7
Solve n÷3 = 9: n÷3×3 = 9×3 → n = 27
Always check: substitute your answer back into the original equation. For x+8=15: 7+8=15 ✓
Common mistake: For "4x=28," don't subtract 4 — 4x means 4×x, so the inverse is division, not subtraction.

Equations in Real Life

1
Read & identify the unknown: "A number plus 9 equals 23." Let the number be n.
2
Write the equation: n + 9 = 23
3
Solve: n = 23 − 9 = 14
4
Check: 14 + 9 = 23 ✓

Practice Questions

1. Solve: x + 9 = 17
2. Solve: y − 5 = 12
3. Solve: 6x = 42
4. Solve: n ÷ 7 = 8
5. Write and solve an equation for: "A number minus 11 equals 19."

🔬 Elements, Compounds & the Periodic Table

What is an Element · Chemical Symbols · Metals vs Non-Metals · Groups & Periods
ElementsPeriodic TableKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
elementA pure substance made of only ONE type of atom.118 known elements.
atomThe smallest particle of an element with that element's properties.Building blocks of matter.
compoundTwo or more elements chemically joined in a fixed ratio.Water = H₂O (hydrogen + oxygen).
chemical symbolA 1-2 letter code for an element. First letter always capital.O=oxygen, Na=sodium, Fe=iron.
periodic tableA chart organising all elements by their properties.Arranged in rows (periods) and columns (groups).
groupA vertical column — similar chemical properties.Group 1 = alkali metals.
periodA horizontal row — same number of electron shells.

Elements, Compounds, Mixtures — The Hierarchy

ELEMENT — one type of atom (e.g. oxygen, O)
+ element →
COMPOUND — water, H₂O (fixed ratio, new properties)
TermWhat It IsExample
ElementPure, one type of atomOxygen, Iron, Gold
CompoundTwo+ elements chemically joinedWater, CO₂, Salt (NaCl)
Mixture (Week 8)Substances combined, not chemically joinedAir, sea water
Key idea: Sodium (Na) is dangerous; chlorine (Cl) is toxic — but together as NaCl, they form harmless table salt! Compounds have entirely new properties.

Metals vs Non-Metals

⚙️ Metals

  • Shiny when polished
  • Good conductors
  • Malleable (bendable)
  • Ductile (drawn into wires)
  • e.g. iron, copper, gold

🧱 Non-Metals

  • Dull appearance
  • Poor conductors
  • Brittle when solid
  • Often gases
  • e.g. oxygen, carbon, sulfur

🏛️ Medieval Europe & the Feudal System

Lords, Serfs, Knights · The Role of the Church · Castles
FeudalismKnightsCastlesKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
feudalismSystem exchanging land for loyalty and service.Organised medieval European society.
monarchA king or queen — supreme ruler.Owned all land in theory.
lord / nobleHigh-rank person who controlled land for the king.Gave land to knights for service.
vassalPerson who received land in exchange for loyalty/service.Could also be a lord to others below.
knightMounted warrior serving a lord, following chivalry.Trained from boyhood; expensive equipment.
serf / peasantWorker bound to a lord's land.Could not leave without permission.
manorA lord's estate including farmland and village.
clergyChurch officials — priests, bishops, monks.The Church was extremely powerful.

The Feudal Pyramid

MONARCH — owns all land
↓ land for loyalty
LORDS / NOBLES — control estates
↓ land for service
KNIGHTS — fight for their lord
↓ protects & rules
SERFS / PEASANTS — farm the land
Key idea: Not an equal exchange — serfs had almost no rights or freedom and could not leave the lord's land.

Knights and Chivalry

AspectDetail
TrainingPage (~age 7) → Squire (~14) → Knight (~21).
EquipmentArmour, warhorse, weapons — very expensive, limited to nobles.
ObligationOwed lord a set number of days of military service yearly (often 40).
ChivalryIdeal code: bravery, loyalty, courtesy, protecting the weak.

Life as a Serf

1
Working the land: Farmed assigned strips, gave part of crops to lord, worked lord's fields for free several days a week.
2
Legal bondage: Could not leave the manor or marry without the lord's permission.
3
Protection in exchange: Received defence and the right to farm a small plot for their own family.

The Power of the Medieval Church

⛪ Religious Role

  • Baptisms, weddings, funerals
  • Ran monasteries — learning, copying books
  • Taught obedience secured salvation

🏛️ Political & Economic Role

  • Owned vast land — more than many kings
  • Collected tithes (1/10 of income)
  • Could crown or excommunicate kings

Castles
