The lesson introduces the concept of "typology" — where a person or event in the Old Testament foreshadows something in the New Testament. In what ways does the lesson say Noah is a "type" of Christ? Identify at least TWO parallels and explain each one. 4 marks

Parallel 1: Noah provided salvation through the ark — Christ provides salvation through himself. 🟢 EasySection A — Subordinating conjunctions and sentence identification.
📚 Subordinating Conjunctions
💡 ESL Tip: Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS) join TWO EQUAL clauses. Subordinating conjunctions (because, although, when, since, unless, while, after, before, if, until, as) introduce a WEAKER clause that DEPENDS on the main clause. The dependent clause alone is an incomplete sentence.
1
List FIVE subordinating conjunctions from the lesson. For each one, write an example sentence showing it in use. 5 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Examples: 1. because — "She stayed inside because it was raining heavily." 2. although — "Although it was late, he kept working." 3. when — "When the lights went out, nobody spoke." 4. unless — "You cannot enter unless you have a ticket." 5. until — "She waited until the last passenger had left." (Many other subordinating conjunctions are acceptable: since, while, after, before, if, as, even though.)

a) CP — coordinating conjunction: for b) CX — subordinating: Until (DC first → comma correctly placed) c) S — no conjunction d) CP — coordinating: yet e) CX — subordinating: While (DC first → comma correctly placed) f) CP — coordinating: but

💡 Show Model Answer ▼

Analyse this professionally written sentence for its structural and punctuation choices: "Although the war had ended three years earlier, the city had not yet found its silence; the wound, it seemed, was not one that time alone could close."

(b) Comma after "earlier" — DC ("Although the war had ended three years earlier") comes first, so comma is required. 🔄 Digestion Journey Retrieval
💡 ESL Tip: "Mechanical digestion" = physically breaking food into smaller pieces (e.g. chewing, churning in the stomach). "Chemical digestion" = enzymes breaking the chemical bonds in food molecules so they become small enough to be absorbed.
1
Describe what happens at EACH stage of digestion. Fill in for each organ: 6 marks
OrganMechanical OR Chemical?What happens here?
Mouth
Oesophagus
Stomach
Small intestine
Large intestine
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Mouth: Both — teeth chew (mechanical); amylase in saliva breaks down starch (chemical). Oesophagus: Mechanical — peristalsis moves food down to the stomach. No digestion. Stomach: Both — churning muscles (mechanical); HCl acid kills bacteria; pepsin (protease) breaks proteins (chemical). Small intestine: Chemical — pancreatic enzymes (amylase, protease, lipase) complete digestion; bile emulsifies fat; nutrients absorbed through villi into blood. Large intestine: Mechanical — water absorbed back into the body; waste (faeces) formed and stored before excretion.
2
What are VILLI? Why are they important for absorption? How is their structure suited to their function? 3 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Villi are tiny finger-like projections that line the wall of the small intestine. They are important because they massively increase the surface area of the intestine, allowing many more nutrients to be absorbed quickly. Their structure suits their function: they are thin-walled (short diffusion distance), richly supplied with blood capillaries (carries nutrients away immediately), and numerous — the total surface area of the small intestine is approximately 250 m² — about the size of a tennis court.

4
Explain why the STOMACH needs to be acidic. What THREE jobs does stomach acid (HCl) perform? Why is the stomach wall not damaged by its own acid? 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
The stomach needs to be acidic (pH 1.5–2) because: Job 1 — Kill bacteria — the acid destroys most harmful microorganisms in food before they can infect the body. Job 2 — Activate pepsin — the enzyme pepsin (protease) is produced inactive (as pepsinogen) and only becomes active in acid conditions. Job 3 — Denature food proteins — acid helps unfold protein structures, making them more accessible for enzyme digestion. The stomach wall is protected by a thick mucus lining that coats the stomach and prevents the acid from digesting the stomach itself. When this mucus is damaged (e.g. by Helicobacter pylori bacteria or certain medicines), it causes a stomach ulcer.
5
The lesson connects digestion (Week 4) to organ systems (Week 3). In what two ways are the DIGESTIVE SYSTEM and CIRCULATORY SYSTEM directly dependent on each other? 3 marks
🔗 Cross-week connection: Week 3 Science — circulatory system (heart + blood vessels), organ systems working together.
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Way 1: The digestive system depends on the circulatory system to carry away absorbed nutrients from the villi — without blood flowing through the capillaries in the villi, nutrients would build up and absorption would stop. Way 2: The circulatory system depends on the digestive system to supply the glucose and amino acids that every body cell needs for energy and repair — without digestion providing these, blood would carry nothing useful to the cells. They are interdependent: neither can function properly without the other.

Evaluate this claim: "Taking vitamin supplements is just as good as eating a balanced diet." Using your scientific knowledge, write a balanced argument (for AND against) and reach a conclusion. Write at least 5 sentences. 6 marks

For: Supplements can correct specific deficiencies effectively — e.g. vitamin D supplements are recommended in the UK for people with limited sun exposure; iron supplements treat anaemia; folic acid supplements are essential in pregnancy. 🔄 Roman Society Retrieval
💡 ESL Tip: "Pax Romana" = Latin for "Roman Peace" — a period of roughly 200 years (27 BC – 180 AD) when the Roman Empire was largely stable and at peace internally. It does not mean NO wars — Rome was constantly fighting on its borders — but within the empire, trade and life were relatively orderly.
1
What was the "Pax Romana"? When did it roughly last? What were TWO benefits of this period for people living in the empire? 3 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
The Pax Romana ("Roman Peace") was a period of roughly 200 years (27 BC – 180 AD) of relative stability and prosperity within the Roman Empire. Benefits: 1. Trade flourished — Roman roads and secure sea routes allowed goods to travel across the entire empire, from Britain to Egypt. 2. Cities grew and were improved — aqueducts, public baths, forums (town squares), and infrastructure were built across the empire, raising the standard of living for many.
2
Describe the Roman social hierarchy. Who was at the top? Who was at the bottom? What group was excluded from full Roman citizenship? 3 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
At the top: Emperor → Patricians (wealthy aristocratic families) → Equites (knights — wealthy but not old aristocracy) → Plebeians (ordinary free Roman citizens) → Freedmen (formerly enslaved people who had been freed) → Slaves (at the bottom — no rights, could be bought and sold). Slaves were excluded from citizenship entirely. Women had limited legal rights compared to men.
3
Name FOUR reasons historians give for the fall of the Western Roman Empire in 476 AD. 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
1. Military pressure — Germanic tribes (Visigoths, Vandals, Huns) continuously attacked the borders and eventually sacked Rome (410 AD — Visigoths; 455 AD — Vandals). 2. Economic problems — endless wars were expensive; high taxes and inflation weakened the economy; trade declined. 3. Political instability — over 50 emperors in the 3rd century alone, many assassinated; civil wars drained military strength. 4. Overextension — the empire was too large to govern and defend effectively; splitting it (West/East, 285 AD) weakened the West especially. (Also acceptable: disease, corruption, moral decline.)
Section B
🟡 MediumSection B — Cause and effect: why empires rise and fall.
⚖️ Rise & Fall Analysis
4
The lesson argues that the very SAME factors that made Rome great also contributed to its fall. Explain this for TWO specific factors — show how each one was a strength AND became a weakness. 4 marks
Example to get you started: Roman roads made trade and military movement fast — but they also allowed invaders to move quickly into the heart of the empire.
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Factor 1: Reliance on enslaved labour — Strength: cheap labour powered Roman farms, mines, and construction, enabling massive wealth and building projects. Weakness: when the supply of slaves from conquest slowed, the economic model collapsed; it also prevented Romans from developing labour-saving technology. Factor 2: A huge, powerful army — Strength: the legions conquered an enormous empire and kept internal order. Weakness: the army became so powerful that soldiers began choosing (and deposing) emperors — creating constant political instability and civil war that exhausted the empire from within.

Compare the fall of Rome (476 AD) to the end of Ancient Greek independence (146 BC — conquered by Rome). What does this pattern suggest about the relationship between civilisations across history? 4 marks
🔗 Cross-week connection: Week 3 Humanities — the end of Ancient Greece (146 BC), legacy of Greek culture, and how one civilisation influences the next.
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Both Greece and Rome eventually fell to outside forces after long periods of dominance. A key similarity: both were weakened by internal division before they were conquered — Greece by wars between city-states (Peloponnesian Wars), Rome by civil wars and political instability. Key difference: when Rome conquered Greece, Greek culture survived and shaped Rome — Roman elites adopted Greek literature, art, philosophy and religion. When Rome fell, its legacy also survived — in law, language, Christianity, and architecture. The pattern suggests that civilisations do not simply disappear — they transform and transmit their ideas into the next dominant culture. No civilisation exists in isolation.

The lesson notes that the Eastern Roman Empire (Byzantine Empire) continued for another 1,000 years after the Western Empire fell in 476 AD. Why do you think historians often focus on the WESTERN fall and call it "the fall of Rome"? Is this historically accurate or misleading? 4 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Historians focus on 476 AD because the city of Rome itself was in the West, and the symbolic end of a Western emperor was deeply significant culturally and politically. However, it IS misleading in several ways: the Eastern Empire continued for nearly 1,000 more years (until 1453 AD — Constantinople fell to the Ottomans). The Byzantines called themselves Romans and preserved Roman law, culture and language. Calling 476 "the fall of Rome" is a Western European perspective that ignores the Eastern Empire's continued existence and influence. A more accurate statement is that 476 AD marks "the end of the Western Roman Empire" — not the end of Rome as a political entity.

"The Roman Empire's greatest achievement was not military conquest but the organisation of diverse peoples under one legal system." Evaluate this claim. Use specific evidence from the lesson. Write at least 6 sentences. 7 marks
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
There is strong evidence for this claim. Roman law was remarkably sophisticated: it introduced concepts of innocent until proven guilty, written public laws, legal representation, and proportional punishment — principles that underpin almost every modern legal system. The empire administered 50–70 million people across 3 continents using standardised law, currency, weights and measures — an extraordinary administrative achievement. Roman citizenship was gradually extended to all free people in the empire (212 AD — Edict of Caracalla) creating a shared legal identity regardless of ethnic origin. However, the claim downplays military conquest — without the legions, there was no empire to administer. Moreover, Roman law was NOT equal: slavery was legal, women had limited rights, and conquered peoples were often brutally subjugated. A balanced evaluation: Roman legal organisation was arguably Rome's most enduring legacy — still active in European legal systems today — but it was built on, and maintained by, military power.

Drawing on your knowledge of Mesopotamia (Week 2), Ancient Greece (Week 3), and Rome (Week 4): write a paragraph arguing that human history shows a pattern of civilisations building on each other. Use at least ONE specific connection between each pair. Write at least 7 sentences. 8 marks
🔗 Cross-week connection: The BIGGEST cross-week question so far. Draw on Weeks 2, 3, and 4 History together.
💡 Show Model Answer ▼
Human history reveals a pattern not of isolated civilisations but of continuous inheritance. Mesopotamia gave the world writing, law codes (Hammurabi), and the concept of organised government — foundations that Egypt also developed independently. Greek thinkers like Aristotle explicitly studied Egyptian and Persian knowledge — Greece did not emerge from a vacuum. When Rome conquered Greece in 146 BC, it absorbed Greek philosophy, architecture, art and religion — Roman gods were largely Greek gods renamed. The Romans then transmitted this combined Graeco-Roman culture across Europe through law, roads, and language (Latin → Romance languages). The fall of Rome in 476 AD did not erase this inheritance — the Catholic Church preserved Latin, Byzantine scholars preserved Greek texts, and Arab scholars preserved and developed Greek mathematics and science. When the Renaissance began in 14th-century Europe, it was partly a rediscovery of classical knowledge — Greek and Roman ideas filtered back into Europe through Islamic scholars. The pattern is clear: each great civilisation stands on the shoulders of those before it, and the ideas of Mesopotamia, Greece, and Rome are still shaping our world today.
