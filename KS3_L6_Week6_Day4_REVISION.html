🌐 Big Ideas That Connect Everything This Week
🔗 Geography shapes everything
Egypt's Nile geography → pharaonic stability (History) → grain surplus → Joseph's role (Bible) → food chain base analogy (Science). The same idea appears in every subject: physical conditions set the stage.
🔗 The base determines everything above
Science: healthy producers support the whole food web. Maths: the original value (base) is what every % calculation starts from. Bible: Joseph's foundational integrity enabled everything that followed. The base matters.
🔗 Roots unlock meaning
English: Latin/Greek roots unlock vocabulary. Science: photo (light) in photosynthesis. Geography: delta from the Greek letter Δ (triangle shape). History: Mesopotamia from Greek meso (middle) + potamos (river). One root, many doors.
🔗 Small changes compound
Maths: compound interest shows small % gains building into large totals. Science: 10% energy loss cascades into dramatic reduction at top. Bible: Joseph's small daily acts of integrity compounded into a character that could handle extraordinary responsibility.

🏆 Final Extended Question
★★
Choose ONE of these essay questions and write a full response (minimum 8 sentences): 15 marks

Option A: "The most important word in Genesis 50:20 is 'intended.' Discuss."
Option B: "Joseph's integrity is more important to the story than his ability to interpret dreams." Do you agree?

💡 Show Marking Guidance ▼
Option A — "intended": Award marks for: the word appears TWICE with different subjects (you intended / God intended), same event, opposite purposes. Option C — providence in hindsight: Award marks for: specific examples from the story where each event only revealed its purpose later (sold → Egypt; imprisoned → meets cupbearer; forgotten → perfect timing before Pharaoh). 🏆 Final Challenge Problem
★★
A second-hand car was bought for £8,000. In year 1 it lost 20% of its value. In year 2 it lost 15% of its remaining value. In year 3 it gained 5% of its value. (a) What was the car's value at the end of each year? (b) What was the overall percentage change from the original £8,000? (c) The owner says "It lost 20%+15%=35% over two years then gained 5%, so overall I lost 30%." Is this correct? Explain why or why not. 8 marks
💡 Show Answer ▼
(a) Year 1: £8,000×0.80 = £6,400. Year 2: £6,400×0.85 = £5,440. Year 3: £5,440×1.05 = £5,712. (b) Change = £8,000−£5,712 = £2,288 loss. % change = 2,288÷8,000×100 = 28.6% overall decrease. (c) No, the owner is incorrect. You cannot simply add and subtract percentages because each percentage is calculated on a different base value. The 15% in year 2 was applied to £6,400 (not £8,000), and the 5% in year 3 was applied to £5,440. Each year's calculation uses that year's starting value. The correct overall change is 28.6%, not 30%. This is the same principle as the "20% up then 20% down" trap from the lesson.

💡 Show Guidance ▼
Grassland example: Grass (producer) → Grasshopper (primary consumer) → Frog (secondary) → Snake (tertiary) → Eagle (quaternary). Also: Grass → Rabbit → Fox → Eagle. Grass → Gazelle → Lion. All dead matter → Fungi/Bacteria (decomposers). Energy: Grass 80,000 kJ → Level 2: 8,000 kJ → Level 3: 800 kJ → Level 4: 80 kJ. Award marks for: correct arrows (direction of energy flow), correct labels, correct 10% calculations at each level.
🏔️ Habitat Match Challenge
★
Match each description to the correct habitat (woodland / coral reef / desert / polar / grassland). Then for each habitat, explain ONE way its physical conditions directly limit what organisms can survive there. 10 marks

a) Warm, shallow, sunlit water with extraordinary species richness
b) Vast open land with seasonal drought; fires can reset the food web
c) Almost no precipitation; extreme temperature swings day to night
d) Freezing temperatures; food chains depend on tiny algae under ice
e) Seasonal; deciduous trees form canopy; complex, multi-layered web
💡 Show Answers ▼
a) Coral reef — ocean warming above ~30°C causes coral bleaching, destroying the photosynthetic algae inside coral and collapsing the producer base. b) Grassland — seasonal drought reduces plant availability, limiting primary consumer numbers; fire can suddenly remove the entire producer layer. c) Desert — near-zero water availability means only specially adapted producers (cacti, sparse scrub) can survive, creating an extremely thin base of the energy pyramid. d) Polar/Tundra — freezing temperatures and long dark winters severely limit photosynthesis, resulting in very short food chains with few species at each level. e) Woodland — seasonal leaf loss in deciduous woodland reduces food availability in winter, forcing many consumers to hibernate, migrate, or switch food sources.

Option B: "Rivers are the most important geographical feature for human civilisation." Argue for or against, using specific evidence from at least 3 sources (History Weeks 2 and 6, Science Week 6).
