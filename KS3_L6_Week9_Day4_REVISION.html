🌐 Big Ideas That Connect Everything This Week
🔗 Structure enables action under pressure
Joshua followed exact, structured instructions to face Jericho. The feudal system gave medieval society structure amid instability. The balancing method gives a reliable structure for solving unknowns. Clear structure turns uncertainty into manageable steps.
🔗 Combining parts creates new properties
Sodium and chlorine combine into safe table salt. Castle walls, moats, and keeps combine into an unbreakable defensive system. Individual points combine into a persuasive presentation. The whole becomes more than the sum of its parts.
🔗 What came before shapes what comes next
Joshua fulfils a promise made generations earlier to Abraham. Feudalism arose from the fall of Rome. An equation's solution depends on correctly identifying the operation first. Understanding the past or the setup determines success in the present.
🔗 Balance and reciprocity
Maths: an equation must stay balanced on both sides. Feudalism: land was exchanged for loyalty — a two-way obligation. Speaking and listening: communication requires both a speaker AND an active listener to actually work.

🏆 Final Challenge Problem
★★
A medieval lord distributes land among three knights. The first knight receives x hectares. The second receives 3 times as much as the first. The third receives 15 hectares more than the first. Combined, the three knights receive 95 hectares. (a) Write expressions for the second and third knights in terms of x. (b) Write and simplify an equation for the total. (c) Solve for x. (d) Find how much land each knight receives. (e) Check your answer. 8 marks
💡 Show Answer ▼
(a) First = x. Second = 3x. Third = x+15. (b) x+3x+(x+15)=95, simplifies to 5x+15=95. (c) 5x=80. x=80÷5=16. (d) First = 16 hectares. Second = 3(16)=48 hectares. Third = 16+15=31 hectares. (e) Check: 16+48+31=95 ✓.

🌍 Cross-Subject Final Challenge
★
Connect this week's science to this week's history: castle walls were often built from stone containing many different mineral compounds. Using the idea that "compounds have entirely new properties different from their component elements," explain in a short paragraph (5-6 sentences) why building materials made of compounds (like stone) might be more useful for construction than pure elements alone. 8 marks
💡 Show Guidance ▼
Most pure elements are unsuitable for building construction on their own: many metals are too soft, too expensive, or too reactive (like sodium, which cannot even touch water safely), while many non-metals are gases at room temperature or too brittle to bear structural weight. Compounds, formed when elements chemically combine, often possess entirely new properties better suited to construction — for example, the calcium compounds found in limestone (used in medieval castle walls) are hard, durable, weather-resistant, and can be shaped into large blocks, none of which describes calcium metal alone (which is actually a soft, reactive metal in its pure form). This mirrors the sodium chloride example from this week's lesson: just as combining two dangerous elements creates a safe, useful compound (table salt), combining elements within rock-forming minerals creates compounds with the hardness, weather-resistance, and structural strength needed for castle construction — properties that emerge specifically from the chemical bonding, not from any single element used alone. This is why medieval builders instinctively used naturally occurring compounds (stone, mortar) rather than attempting to construct with pure elemental metals, even where available.
