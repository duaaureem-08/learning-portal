📖 Noah — Faith & Obedience

Genesis 6–9 · The Flood · God's Covenant · Trusting God Before You See the Result
Hebrews 11:7Genesis 6–9CovenantKS3 Level 6

Memory Verse

"By faith Noah, when warned about things not yet seen, in holy fear built an ark to save his family. By his faith he condemned the world and became heir of the righteousness that comes by faith."
Hebrews 11:7 — NIV
Word-by-word: "warned about things not yet seen" = faith trusts God's word BEFORE there is proof. "in holy fear" = Noah took the warning seriously, with respect. "built an ark" = real faith leads to ACTION. "condemned the world" = his righteous life quietly contrasted with everyone else's. "heir of righteousness" = he received right standing with God BECAUSE of his faith.

Key Vocabulary

arkA huge boat built to survive a flood. Hebrew "tebah" = box/chest.Built for survival, not for sailing or exploring.
covenantA solemn, binding PROMISE — often between God and people.God's covenant with Noah was a promise to the whole earth.
righteousLiving in right relationship with God; doing what is right.Noah was righteous compared to his generation.
corruptMorally rotten — full of violence and evil.Describes the world before the flood (Gen 6:5).

obedienceDoing what God commands — even if difficult or slow.Noah obeyed even though building took a very long time.

Noah vs His Generation

🌍 Noah's Generation

  • Violence and corruption spread across the whole earth
  • Every inclination of people's hearts turned toward evil
  • No one else is described as living for God

🙏 Noah

  • Described as "righteous" and "blameless" (Gen 6:9)
  • "Walked faithfully with God" — close relationship
  • Chosen to carry forward God's purposes
Remember: God's judgment was a response to a long pattern of corruption — and God ALWAYS provided a way of escape (the ark). Judgment and mercy appear TOGETHER in this story.

The Flood — Order of Events

1
Day 1: Rain begins, falls for 40 days and 40 nights (Gen 7:11–12). Noah, his family (8 people) and pairs of animals are inside the ark.
2
Days 1–150: Floodwaters rise and cover even the highest mountains (Gen 7:19–20). Waters remain high for 150 days.
3
~Month 7: The ark rests on Mount Ararat (Gen 8:4) as the waters recede.
4
Final days: Noah sends out a raven, then a dove. The dove returns with an olive leaf — a sign that new life had begun (Gen 8:6–12).

The Covenant & Its Sign

After the flood, God makes a covenant with Noah, every living creature, and the earth itself (Gen 9:8–17): He will never again destroy the earth with a flood.

Ordinary PromiseBiblical Covenant
Who?Usually two peopleGod and people (sometimes all creation)
How binding?Can be broken/forgottenPermanent — never withdrawn
Sign?Not usuallyYes — a visible reminder (rainbow)
🌈 The sign: Genesis 9:13 — God sets the rainbow in the sky as the sign of this covenant. This is the FIRST of several major covenants in the Bible (later: Abraham, Moses, David, and the New Covenant through Jesus).

✏️ Sentence Types & Punctuation

Simple, Compound and Complex Sentences · Clauses, Conjunctions, Commas
ClausesConjunctionsPunctuationKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
clauseA group of words with a SUBJECT and a VERB."The dog barked." → subject = the dog, verb = barked.
independent clauseA clause that can stand ALONE as a sentence (main clause)."The dog barked." makes complete sense alone.
dependent clauseA clause that CANNOT stand alone (subordinate clause)."...because it was hungry" needs more information.
conjunctionA word that JOINS clauses or words together.and, but, because, although...
coordinating conjunctionJoins TWO independent clauses (FANBOYS).For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So
subordinating conjunctionBegins a DEPENDENT clause.because, although, when, if, since, while
simple sentenceONE independent clause — can be short or long."The students finished their homework on time."
compound sentenceTWO+ independent clauses joined by FANBOYS or ; "I finished early, so I went outside."
complex sentenceIndependent clause + dependent clause(s)."Although it rained, we walked to school."

What Is a Clause? — The Test

Ask two questions about a group of words:

1
Does it have a SUBJECT (who/what) and a VERB (action)? If yes → it's a clause.
2
Does it make COMPLETE sense alone? If YES → independent clause. If NO → dependent clause.
"The dog barked." → subject + verb, makes sense alone → independent.
"...because it was hungry." → subject + verb, but NOT complete alone → dependent.
Combine: "The dog barked because it was hungry." = one independent + one dependent = complex sentence.

Diagram — Three Sentence Types Compared

Simple · Compound · Complex
TypeStructureExample
SIMPLEOne independent clause"Maya studied hard for her exam."
COMPOUNDIndependent + comma + FANBOYS + Independent"I wanted to play, but it started raining."
COMPLEXDependent clause + Independent clause (or reverse)"Although she was tired, she finished her homework."
ESL tip: Length does NOT decide the type — STRUCTURE does. A long sentence can still be "simple" if it has only one independent clause.

FANBOYS — Joining Equal Ideas

WordMeaningExample
ForGives a reasonShe studied hard, for the exam was important.
AndAdds an equal ideaMaya finished homework, and she practised piano.
NorAdds a second negativeHe did not complain, nor did he give up.
ButShows contrastI wanted to play, but it started raining.
OrShows a choiceWe can revise today, or tomorrow.
YetShows surprising contrastThe test was hard, yet she passed easily.
SoShows a resultIt rained heavily, so the match was cancelled.
Rule: independent clause + comma + FANBOYS word + independent clause.

Subordinating Conjunctions — Two Orders, Two Comma Rules

Dependent clause FIRST → comma

  • "Although she was tired, she finished her homework."
  • "Because it was raining, the match was postponed."
  • "When the bell rang, the students lined up."

Independent clause FIRST → usually no comma

  • "She finished her homework although she was tired."
  • "The match was postponed because it was raining."
  • "The students lined up when the bell rang."

Common subordinating conjunctions: because, although, when, if, since, while, after, before, unless, until, even though, as soon as.

Punctuation Rules — Quick Reference

1
Full stop ( . ) — ends a statement. "The library closes at 5pm."
2
Question mark ( ? ) — ends a DIRECT question only. "What time does it close?" (NOT for "She asked what time it closes.")
3
Comma in a list — "I packed a pencil, a ruler, a calculator and a book."
4
Comma after introductory phrase — "However, the results were surprising."
5
Comma before FANBOYS (compound sentences) — "I finished my work, so I went outside."
6
Comma after leading dependent clause (complex sentences) — "Although it was late, she kept working."

Common Mistake — The Comma Splice

Comma splice: joining TWO independent clauses with ONLY a comma — no conjunction. One of the most common KS3 errors!
❌ Incorrect✅ Correct
I went to the shop, I bought some bread.I went to the shop, and I bought some bread.
I went to the shop, I bought some bread.I went to the shop. I bought some bread.
I went to the shop, I bought some bread.I went to the shop; I bought some bread.
Self-check: Read each half separately. If BOTH halves make complete sense alone, you need a conjunction, semicolon, or full stop — never just a comma.

Adding — UNLIKE Denominators (Find LCM First)

You CANNOT add fractions with different denominators directly! 1/4 + 1/6 ≠ 2/10
1
Find the LCM: multiples of 4 = 4,8,12,16... multiples of 6 = 6,12,18... → LCM(4,6)=12
2
Convert each fraction: 1/4 → ×3 → 3/12. 1/6 → ×2 → 2/12.
3
Add numerators: 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12.
4
Simplify: HCF(5,12)=1 → already simplest. Final answer: 5/12

Subtracting Fractions — Same Method

Like denominators: 7/9 − 4/9 = 3/9 = 1/3 (simplify! HCF(3,9)=3)

Unlike denominators: 5/6 − 1/4 → LCM(6,4)=12 → 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12

Order matters! 5/6 − 1/4 is NOT the same as 1/4 − 5/6. Always subtract in the order written.

Mixed Numbers — Adding

Method: Add whole numbers together. Add fraction parts (common denominator if needed). If fraction part ≥1, convert and carry the extra whole number.
2¾ + 1½ = ?

Whole numbers: 2 + 1 = 3

Fractions: 3/4 + 1/2 → LCM(4,2)=4 → 3/4 + 2/4 = 5/4

5/4 is improper → 5/4 = 1¼

Combine: 3 + 1¼ = 4¼

Mixed Numbers — Subtracting with Borrowing

When to borrow: when the fraction you are SUBTRACTING is bigger than the fraction you START with.
1
Example: 4⅕ − 1⅗. Compare fractions: 1/5 < 3/5 → need to borrow.
2
Borrow 1 from the whole number: 4⅕ = 3 + 5/5 + 1/5 = 3 6/5
3
Subtract whole numbers: 3−1=2. Subtract fractions: 6/5−3/5=3/5.
4
Combine: 2 + 3/5 = 2⅗. (Check: HCF(3,5)=1, already simplified.)
Alternative method: Convert both to improper fractions first: 4⅕=21/5, 1⅗=8/5. 21/5−8/5=13/5=2⅗ ✓ Same answer.

The Five Food Groups — What Your Body Needs

Food GroupMain FunctionHalal-Friendly Examples
CarbohydratesMain energy source — largest part of mealsRice, roti/chapati, bread, pasta, potatoes, oats
Fruits & VegetablesVitamins, minerals & fibre — support immunity & digestionApples, bananas, spinach, carrots, tomatoes, oranges

Remember: A balanced diet does NOT mean equal amounts of everything. It means the RIGHT PROPORTIONS — much more carbohydrates and fruit/veg, much less fat.

Macronutrients vs Micronutrients

Macronutrients — large amounts

  • Carbohydrates: energy → broken into glucose
  • Proteins: growth/repair → broken into amino acids
  • Fats: energy storage, cell membranes → broken into fatty acids + glycerol

Micronutrients — tiny amounts

  • Vitamin C: immune system, wound healing (citrus, peppers)
  • Vitamin D: calcium absorption, bone health (sunlight, eggs)
  • Calcium: strong bones and teeth (dairy, leafy greens)
  • Iron: makes red blood cells (lentils, spinach, red meat) — deficiency → anaemia

Diagram — The Journey of Food Through the Digestive System

Follow the food — 5 key stages
1
👄 Mouth: Teeth = mechanical digestion (chewing). Saliva contains amylase → starts breaking starch into sugars (chemical digestion). Produces a soft ball of food called a bolus.
2
⬇️ Oesophagus: A muscular tube. Peristalsis (wave-like muscle squeezing) pushes the bolus down to the stomach. No digestion happens here — just transport.
3
🟡 Stomach: Muscular bag. Hydrochloric acid (pH ~2) + pepsin (a protease enzyme) break down PROTEINS. Churning produces a liquid called chyme. Acid also kills bacteria.
4
〰️ Small Intestine (~6 m): Main site of digestion AND absorption. Receives bile (emulsifies fats) from liver/gallbladder, and enzymes from the pancreas (amylase, protease, lipase). Nutrients absorbed into blood through villi (finger-like projections — massive surface area).
5
🌀 Large Intestine: Absorbs remaining WATER and minerals. Beneficial bacteria break down some remaining material. Remaining waste forms faeces → stored in rectum → egested.

Enzymes — Three Key Ones to Know

Bile ≠ enzyme: Bile is produced by the liver and stored in the gallbladder. It emulsifies fats — breaking large fat globules into tiny droplets — which gives lipase a much greater surface area to work on. It does not break molecules apart chemically.

🏛️ The Roman Empire

Rise and Fall · Republic vs Empire · Roman Britain · Legacy to the Modern World
753 BC–476 ADRoman BritainGovernmentKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample / Note
empireA large territory of many different lands ruled by ONE authority.The Roman Empire stretched from Britain to Egypt to Iraq.
republicA system where REPRESENTATIVES are elected to govern.Rome was a republic for ~500 years before becoming an empire.
emperorThe supreme ruler of the Roman Empire.Augustus was the first emperor (27 BC).
senateA council of experienced politicians who advised and governed Rome.Under the Republic, the Senate held real power.

Republic vs Empire — and a Look Back at Ancient Greece (Week 3)

FeatureRoman Republic (509–27 BC)Roman Empire (27 BC onward)
Who holds power?Two elected Consuls (one-year terms) + SenateOne Emperor — often for life
The SenateReal political power — debated laws, controlled financesMostly advisory — real power with the emperor

The Roman Army — Key Facts

1
The Legion: ~5,000 soldiers, divided into centuries (~80 men each), each led by a centurion.
2
Discipline and Training: Constant drilling, marching and camp-building — echoes Spartan military culture from Week 3 (but for very different reasons).
3
Roman Roads: Built for fast military movement — many routes still used today in Britain.
4
Scale: At its height, ~5 million km² — from Britain to Egypt to Iraq.

Roman Britain — How Rome Changed Britain

What Rome Built

  • Roads: straight, long-lasting — several modern routes follow them
  • Hadrian's Wall (~122 AD) — 117 km, still partly standing today
  • Towns: Londinium (London), Aquae Sulis (Bath), Eboracum (York), Deva (Chester)
  • Technology: underfloor heating (hypocausts), public baths, running water

When Rome Left (410 AD)

  • Roman forces withdrew as the empire faced pressure elsewhere
  • Ended nearly 400 years of Roman rule in Britain
  • Advanced technology (baths, underfloor heating) disappeared for centuries
  • Local rulers left to manage their own defence — a major turning point

Why Did Rome Fall? — Four Key Causes

CauseExplanation
Economic troublesHeavy taxation, inflation, and rising costs of the army weakened the economy.
Military pressureGroups such as the Goths, Vandals and Huns attacked Rome's borders from multiple directions.
Political instabilityFrequent changes of emperor (sometimes by assassination or civil war) prevented long-term stable government.
SizeThe empire became so vast it was split into Western and Eastern halves — weakening unity.
Important: The EASTERN Roman Empire (Byzantine, centred on Constantinople) continued until 1453 AD — nearly 1,000 years after the Western empire fell in 476 AD. "The fall of Rome" means the fall of the WESTERN half only.

Roman Legacy — Still Visible Today

LegacyHow It Affects the World Today
LanguageLatin is the ancestor of Italian, Spanish, French, Portuguese and Romanian. Huge amounts of English vocabulary comes from Latin. The Latin alphabet is the writing system you are using RIGHT NOW.
LawMany modern legal systems (especially in Europe) trace principles to Roman law — written codes, consistent application, the idea of legal rights.
EngineeringThe arch, concrete, aqueducts. The Pantheon in Rome (built ~125 AD) still stands — its concrete dome was the largest in the world for over 1,300 years.
The CalendarJuly = Julius Caesar. August = Augustus. The Gregorian calendar we use today is based on the Roman Julian calendar. "December" comes from decem (Latin for ten — it was originally the 10th month).
Government"Republic" comes from Latin res publica (public affair). Later thinkers designing new governments (e.g. 1700s) looked to Rome's republic as a model for elected representatives and limits on single-person power.
Week 3 connection: Both Ancient Greece AND Rome influenced Western civilisation. Greece gave us democracy, philosophy and science. Rome gave us law, engineering, language and the structure of government. Neither influence replaced the other — they built on each other.
