📖 Abraham — Called to Trust God

Genesis 12 · God's Call · Leaving Home · Faith and Obedience Continued
Genesis 12:2Genesis 12–13CovenantKS3 Level 6

Memory Verse

"I will make you into a great nation, and I will bless you; I will make your name great, and you will be a blessing."
Genesis 12:2 — NIV
Word-by-word: "great nation" = an enormous number of descendants — astonishing for a man with no children yet. "I will bless you" = God's favour resting on Abram's life. "make your name great" = lasting honour, not fame for its own sake. "you will be a blessing" = the promise flows OUTWARD to other people, not just to Abram himself.

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample / Note

Where Did Abraham Come From?

Abram lived in Ur of the Chaldeans — a wealthy, advanced city in Mesopotamia (modern-day southern Iraq), full of temples to many different gods.

What Abram Left Behind

  • A settled home in an established, wealthy city
  • His wider family and extended household
  • A familiar culture and way of life
  • Security and certainty about the future

What He Was Promised Instead

  • A land he had not yet seen
  • Descendants forming a "great nation," though childless
  • God's blessing and protection on the journey
  • A role in blessing "all peoples on earth"
KS3 thinking point: Abram was 75 years old (Genesis 12:4) when he left — giving up a settled life late in life, based on a promise with no visible proof yet.

The Call of Abraham — Genesis 12:1–3

The Command + The Promise

The Command: "Go from your country, your people and your father's household to the land I will show you."

The Promise: "I will make you into a great nation... I will bless you... you will be a blessing... all peoples on earth will be blessed through you."

Notice the order: God asks Abram to obey FIRST — leave everything — and only afterward explains the destination. Trust comes before full information, just as with Noah.

Abram's Journey — From Ur to Canaan

1
Ur of the Chaldeans: Abram's birthplace in Mesopotamia. His father Terah first leads the family out, intending to go to Canaan.
2
Harran: The family settles for a time; Terah dies there. God repeats His call directly to Abram, now 75 years old.
3
Canaan — The Promised Land: Abram, Sarai and Lot arrive (Gen 12:5–6). God promises this land to his descendants. Abram builds an altar.
4
A Detour to Egypt: A severe famine forces Abram to Egypt temporarily (Gen 12:10) — even a man of faith faces real hardship.
Map skills link: Ur to Harran to Canaan covers roughly 1,000 miles (1,600 km), mostly on foot or by caravan. See this week's History lesson on maps and geographic tools!

Comparing Two Examples of Faith — Noah and Abraham

FeatureNoah (Week 4)Abraham (Week 5)

Length of obedienceDecades of construction before rain fellA lifetime as a nomad

The pattern continues: Both stories show the same golden idea — faith means trusting and obeying God's word BEFORE you can see how it will turn out.

✏️ Descriptive Writing

ImageryFigurative LanguageShow, Don't TellKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
descriptive writingWriting that creates a vivid picture in the reader's mind.Uses sensory detail and figurative language.
imageryLanguage that appeals to the FIVE SENSES.Sight, sound, smell, taste, touch.
figurative languageLanguage that means something BEYOND its literal meaning.Similes, metaphors, personification, hyperbole.
literal languageLanguage that means exactly what it says."The fire was hot."
simileA comparison using "like" or "as"."The fire roared like a hungry lion."
metaphorStates one thing IS another — no "like/as"."The fire was a hungry lion."
personificationGiving human qualities to non-human things."The wind whispered through the trees."
hyperboleExtreme exaggeration used for effect."I've told you a million times."
sensory detailDescription drawing on one or more of the five senses.Makes writing feel real and immediate.

Literal vs Descriptive — Why Detail Matters

The test: Could a reader picture, hear, smell, taste, or feel what you are describing? Vague facts are LITERAL. Sensory detail is DESCRIPTIVE.
❌ Literal (Flat)✅ Descriptive (Vivid)
The room was messy.Crumpled papers spilled across the desk, and the bin overflowed with crushed cans.
The food smelled good.The smell of cinnamon and fresh bread drifted from the kitchen.
It was cold outside.An icy wind bit at her fingers and stung her cheeks.

The Five Senses — Building a Sensory Toolkit

Choose senses that suit the scene

👀 Sight: "The sky turned a deep shade of orange as the sun dipped below the hills."

👂 Sound: "Leaves crunched underfoot, and somewhere a dog barked twice."

👃 Smell: "The sharp tang of salt water filled the air near the harbour."

👅 Taste: "The lemon was so sour her whole mouth puckered."

✋ Touch: "The sand was rough and still warm from the afternoon sun."

KS3 key idea: Strong descriptive writing deliberately chooses TWO or THREE senses that suit the scene — rather than relying only on sight, the most overused sense in student writing.

Four Figurative Devices — Quick Reference

DeviceDefinitionExample
SimileComparison using "like" or "as""Her eyes were as bright as stars."
MetaphorStates one thing IS another"Time is a thief."
PersonificationHuman qualities given to non-human things"The old house groaned under the storm."
HyperboleExtreme exaggeration for effect"This bag weighs a tonne."
ESL tip: Personification only works on things that are NOT human. If you describe an actual person's real action, it is not personification — just normal description.

Show, Don't Tell — Strengthening Description

❌ Telling✅ Showing
She was nervous.Her hands trembled, and she kept glancing at the door.
The dog was angry.The dog's lips curled back, and a low growl rumbled in its throat.
The forest was scary.Twisted branches clawed at the darkening sky, and every snapped twig echoed like a gunshot.
Precise word choice: Instead of "big," try enormous, towering, vast. Instead of "good," try delicious, magnificent, triumphant. Precise words beat piling up weak adjectives.

Building a Descriptive Paragraph — Step by Step

1
Choose a clear focus: one scene or moment, not a whole day.
2
Select two or three senses that suit the scene best.
3
Add ONE simile or metaphor — don't overload with comparisons.
4
Replace vague adjectives ("nice," "big," "good") with precise ones.
5
Read it aloud and vary your sentence lengths for impact.

Practice Questions

1. Write one LITERAL sentence, then rewrite it as a DESCRIPTIVE sentence using sensory detail.
2. Write one SIMILE and one METAPHOR describing the same thing (e.g. a thunderstorm).
3. Write one sentence using PERSONIFICATION to describe the weather.
4. Rewrite "The food was good" using show, don't tell.
5. Choose two senses and write a two-sentence description of your school playground.
6. Why do writers usually limit themselves to ONE simile or metaphor per paragraph?
7. Write a short descriptive paragraph (3-4 sentences) about a place you know well, using at least one figurative device.

🧮 Multiplying & Dividing Fractions

Fraction × Fraction · Cross-Cancelling · Mixed Numbers · Dividing Using Reciprocals
ReciprocalsCross-CancelMixed NumbersKS3 Level 6

Key Vocabulary

WordWhat It MeansExample
numeratorThe TOP number of a fraction.In 3/4, the numerator is 3.
denominatorThe BOTTOM number of a fraction.In 3/4, the denominator is 4.
reciprocalA fraction FLIPPED upside down.Reciprocal of 2/3 is 3/2.
cross-cancelSimplifying common factors before multiplying.Makes numbers smaller before you start.
improper fractionNumerator LARGER than denominator.7/4 — convert mixed numbers to this first.
mixed numberA whole number + a fraction.2¾ — must convert before multiplying/dividing.
unit fractionA fraction with numerator of 1.1/2, 1/3, 1/8.

Multiplying Fractions — Straight Across

Rule: multiply numerators together, multiply denominators together. You do NOT need a common denominator to multiply.
2/3 × 1/4 = 1/6

a/b × c/d = (a×c)/(b×d)

2/3 × 1/4 = (2×1)/(3×4) = 2/12 = 1/6

Step 1 — Take 1/4 of the whole
1/4
Step 2 — Now take 2/3 of THAT piece
2/3
= 2/12 of the whole = 1/6
"Of" means multiply: "2/3 of 1/4" and "2/3 × 1/4" mean the same thing.

Simplify BEFORE You Multiply — Cross-Cancelling

1
Look diagonally across the two fractions for shared factors.
2
For 4/9 × 3/8: 4 and 8 share a factor of 4 (→1/2). 3 and 9 share a factor of 3 (→1/3).
3
Now multiply the simplified fractions: 1/3 × 1/2 = 1/6.
4
Check: 12/72 simplified (HCF=12) also equals 1/6 — cross-cancelling just uses smaller numbers.

Multiplying Mixed Numbers — Convert First!

Common mistake: you CANNOT multiply whole numbers and fraction parts separately. Convert to improper fractions FIRST.
2½ × 1⅓ = 3⅓

Convert: 2½ = 5/2    1⅓ = 4/3

Multiply: 5/2 × 4/3 = 20/6

Simplify: 20/6 = 10/3 = 3⅓

Dividing Fractions — Keep, Change, Flip

The golden rule: KEEP the first fraction, CHANGE ÷ to ×, FLIP the second fraction (find its reciprocal). Then multiply.
1
For 3/4 ÷ 2/5: KEEP 3/4 exactly as it is.
2
CHANGE ÷ to ×: 3/4 × ...
3
FLIP 2/5 to its reciprocal 5/2: 3/4 × 5/2.
4
Multiply: 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = 1⅞

Why Does "Keep, Change, Flip" Work?

Dividing by a fraction answers: "How many of that fraction fit inside the first number?"

4 ÷ 1/2 = 8

How many halves fit into 4 whole pizzas? Each pizza = 2 halves, so 4 × 2 = 8 halves.

Using Keep, Change, Flip: 4/1 × 2/1 = 8/1 = 8 ✓ Same answer!

KS3 key idea: Dividing by a fraction smaller than 1 always makes the answer BIGGER — because you're asking how many small pieces fit into a bigger amount.

Dividing Mixed Numbers

Exactly like multiplying — convert to improper fractions FIRST, then apply Keep, Change, Flip.

3¼ ÷ 1½ = 2⅙

Convert: 3¼ = 13/4    1½ = 3/2

Keep, Change, Flip: 13/4 × 2/3 → cross-cancel → 13/2 × 1/3 = 13/6 = 2⅙

The Photosynthesis Equation

Word and symbol equations

Word equation: carbon dioxide + water --(light energy)--> glucose + oxygen

Symbol equation: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O --(light, chlorophyll)--> C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

TermRoleWhere It Comes From
Carbon dioxideReactantAbsorbed from the air via stomata
WaterReactantAbsorbed by roots, carried up by xylem
Light energyPowers the reactionSunlight, absorbed by chlorophyll
GlucoseProduct — the plant's foodUsed for energy, growth, or stored
OxygenProduct — released as waste gasReleased via stomata

Why Plants Need Light

KS3 key concept: Plants are "producers" — they make their own food using light energy. This is the very first step of almost every food chain on Earth.

Without light, photosynthesis stops almost immediately — plants kept in darkness turn pale and eventually die.

Leaf Structure — The Journey of Light, Water and Gas

From top surface down to the bottom of the leaf
1
☀️ Upper Epidermis: Transparent cells with a waxy cuticle — protects the leaf while letting maximum light through.
2
🟢 Palisade Mesophyll: Tightly packed cells with MANY chloroplasts — the main site of photosynthesis.
3
🫧 Spongy Mesophyll: Loosely packed cells with air spaces — allows gases to diffuse freely.
4
🔲 Stomata & Guard Cells: Tiny pores (mostly lower surface) controlling gas exchange and water loss.
5
➰ Veins (Xylem & Phloem): Xylem brings water IN; phloem carries glucose OUT to the rest of the plant.

Limiting Factors — What Slows Photosynthesis Down?

Limiting FactorEffect If Too LowReal-World Example
Light intensityLess energy to power the reactionPlants in deep shade grow slowly

Plant Cells vs Animal Cells — Review & Extension

🌱 Plant Cells — Extra Structures

  • Cell wall: rigid, made of cellulose — support
  • Chloroplasts: site of photosynthesis
  • Large permanent vacuole: stores water, rigidity

🐾 Shared by Both

  • Nucleus: controls cell activities
  • Cytoplasm: where reactions occur
  • Cell membrane: controls entry/exit
  • Mitochondria: site of respiration

Why Photosynthesis Matters

1
Produces the oxygen we breathe.
2
Foundation of almost every food chain — plants are "producers."
3
Created fossil fuels from ancient plant remains.
4
Removes carbon dioxide from the air — why deforestation is a climate concern.

Memory tip: LATitude = LADDER (flat, horizontal rungs). LONGitude is LONG, stretching top to bottom.

Reading Coordinates — Finding an Exact Location

1
Coordinates are always written LATITUDE FIRST, then longitude.
2
Each number includes a direction letter, e.g. London ≈ 51.5°N, 0.1°W.
3
Find the latitude line first, tracing N or S of the Equator.
4
Then find the longitude line E or W of the Prime Meridian — where they cross is your location.
Bible connection: Ur of the Chaldeans (Abram's homeland) sits at approximately 30.9°N, 46.1°E, while Canaan lies around 31.5°N, 35°E — plotting Abram's journey shows how far he travelled.

Map Scale — Real World vs Paper

Scale 1 : 50,000

1 cm on the map = 50,000 cm in real life = 500 m

So 6 cm on the map = 6 × 500 m = 3,000 m = 3 km in real life

Other ways scale is shown: as a scale BAR drawn on the map, or in WORDS ("1 cm represents 1 km"). All describe the same relationship.

Types of Maps — Choosing the Right Tool

⛰️ TopographicElevation using contour lines

Globe vs Flat Map — Why Are They Different?

🌐 Globe

  • Most ACCURATE 3D model of Earth
  • Can't see the whole world at once
  • Not practical to carry around

🗺️ Flat Map

  • Practical and portable
  • Always involves some DISTORTION
  • Different projections distort different things
Mercator projection example: Greenland appears similar in size to Africa — but Africa is actually about 14 times larger! Flattening a sphere always stretches something.
